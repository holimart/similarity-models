# Build trustworthy invoice and receipt extraction

The strongest practical approach to invoice and receipt understanding is a **hybrid pipeline**: recover text and geometry, extract semantic fields and line-item relationships, normalize with locale-aware code, then validate and review uncertain or financially consequential results. Hosted document processors are the fastest route to a useful baseline; open-weight OCR, layout models and vision-language models (VLMs) offer greater control and can fit a single GPU, while large hosted multimodal LLMs are flexible for variable layouts and exception handling. Yet public scores do not establish which product reads a given invoice best: receipt, VQA, OCR and PDF-parsing benchmarks measure different tasks, and there is no credible common field-level bake-off of current systems. **For Czech účtenky, preserve full Czech diacritics and validate on local thermal receipts; published end-to-end evidence is particularly sparse, and broad “European language” claims are not enough.** Select on a representative, vendor-held-out test set and measure cost per accepted, correct record—not model score or API fee alone. Pricing and model claims below are snapshots checked **26 September 2026**; live prices, versions and terms should be rechecked before procurement.

## Separate recognition, structure and business meaning

Document extraction is a chain of distinct problems: image quality and orientation; text recognition; reading order and spatial structure; semantic assignment to fields; line-item row/column linking; locale normalization; and business validation. SROIE explicitly distinguishes text localization, OCR and key-information extraction, illustrating why a single “accuracy” number hides where errors arise. ([SROIE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10213)) A clear OCR transcript can still map “subtotal” to “total,” while a correct field label can be paired with a misread digit. Preserve raw text, boxes, page references, confidence signals and normalized values so these failure classes remain diagnosable.

| Method family | What it does | Strengths | Main trade-offs / best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Native PDF text + rules | Reads embedded text where present; applies labels, geometry, templates, regex, locale parsing and validations. | Cheap, explainable and highly controllable on stable formats; avoids unnecessary OCR. | Layout variation, ambiguous labels and template drift create brittle rules; line items need explicit grouping. Best for known vendors or as a deterministic post-processing layer. |
| OCR + rules / managed document API | Recognizes words and geometry, then uses heuristics or a pretrained invoice/expense schema. | Auditable intermediate evidence; dedicated processors return common headers, totals and often line items. | OCR and parser errors compound; field schemas and language support differ. Good baseline and low-ops path. |
| Sequence tagging and layout-aware models | Assigns semantic labels to OCR tokens using sequence context, 2-D boxes and sometimes page pixels; relation models link labels, values and rows. | Efficient, task-specific, fine-tunable, often more structured and inspectable than free-form generation. | Requires OCR, annotations and model operations; table association and unseen layouts remain hard. Suitable for stable schemas and controlled data. |
| OCR-free document models | Encodes page images and generates structured sequences, e.g. Donut. | Avoids a separate OCR handoff and can emit nested item structures directly. | Autoregressive output can be malformed or confidently wrong; resolution and output length affect cost; evidence localization is less direct. Fine-tune and validate for the target schema. |
| General VLM / multimodal LLM | Reads page images directly and responds to schema-directed prompts; can reason over unusual layouts and multiple cues. | Flexible, rapid to prototype, useful for long-tail semantics and exception resolution. | Image billing/compute varies; small print and line-item precision remain uncertain; valid JSON is not verified truth. Requires evidence, checks and review. |
| Document parsers (Markdown/JSON layout) | Converts PDFs/images into text, tables, blocks and spatial structures, usually without a guaranteed invoice schema. | Useful reusable front-end for downstream extraction and document search. | Page reconstruction quality is not invoice-field accuracy; forms and financial semantics often need another extraction stage. |

Classical OCR remains relevant. Tesseract provides configurable segmentation, multiple outputs and broad language models; its guidance recommends adequate resolution, deskewing and image cleanup and warns that tables need custom layout work. ([Tesseract quality guide](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/ImproveQuality.html); [Tesseract repository](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesseract)) PaddleOCR offers modular orientation, detection and recognition components; its published module timings exclude preprocessing and post-processing, so they are not whole-document service latency. ([PaddleOCR OCR pipeline](https://www.paddleocr.ai/main/en/version3.x/pipeline_usage/OCR.html)) OCR should not be applied blindly to text-native PDFs: extract their text layer first and use OCR only for image pages or fallback.

Supervised approaches evolved from CRF/BiLSTM sequence taggers to token classifiers with 2-D layout and image features. CloudScan evaluated 326,471 invoices and reported average F1 of 0.891 on seen layouts and 0.840 on unseen layouts for its recurrent model; this historical result illustrates both scale and the generalization gap, not a current model ranking. ([CloudScan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)) LayoutLM jointly represents text and spatial position; LayoutLMv2/v3 add stronger multimodal image-layout interaction, while BROS emphasizes relative spatial relationships without image features and LiLT targets language-independent layout transfer. ([LayoutLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13318); [LayoutLMv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14740); [LayoutLMv3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08387); [BROS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.04539); [LiLT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13669)) Use token tagging where OCR is sound and fields are stable; use relation prediction or table structure recognition when the output depends on which quantity, unit price and amount belong to which row. Table Transformer, for example, detects table/cell structure but still needs OCR or PDF text to populate cells. ([Table Transformer](https://github.com/microsoft/table-transformer)) No cited evidence isolates relation modeling’s invoice-specific benefit in a controlled head-to-head test.

Donut’s image-to-sequence approach reports CORD receipt field F1 84.1 and tree-edit-distance accuracy 90.9, versus the paper’s OCR-based LayoutLMv2 baseline at 78.9 and 82.4 respectively. These are results in the authors’ setup, not a controlled general-invoice comparison; CORD is a small Indonesian receipt benchmark. Donut’s paper notes that missing even one character can fail a field under exact-sensitive scoring and that higher image resolution can help tiny text at extra compute. ([Donut paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15664); [CORD](https://github.com/clovaai/cord)) General parsers such as Docling, Marker, MinerU, olmOCR, Nanonets-OCR2 and PaddleOCR-VL produce layouts, text, Markdown or structured blocks; downstream schema extraction is still needed unless a dedicated KIE mode is used. ([Docling](https://github.com/docling-project/docling); [Marker](https://github.com/datalab-to/marker); [MinerU](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU); [olmOCR](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr); [Nanonets-OCR2](https://huggingface.co/nanonets/Nanonets-OCR2-3B); [PaddleOCR](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR))

## Choose hosted processing or an open-weight GPU by operational fit

Hosted specialist processors are usually the quickest baseline. AWS Textract AnalyzeExpense returns summary and line-item groups, labels/values, confidence and geometry; Azure Document Intelligence has dedicated invoice and receipt models with structured fields; Google Document AI offers Invoice and Expense Parser; ABBYY Vantage is a broader configurable IDP and workflow platform. ([AWS AnalyzeExpense](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/analyzing-document-expense.html); [Azure invoice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/invoice?view=doc-intel-4.0.0); [Azure receipt](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/receipt?view=doc-intel-4.0.0); [Google processors](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/processors-list); [ABBYY Vantage](https://www.abbyy.com/vantage/)) API schemas, confidence signals and feature claims are vendor-authored; no shared independent benchmark establishes a general accuracy winner. Enterprise workflow vendors such as Rossum, Nanonets, Veryfi, Mindee and Doxis/Klippa may bundle validation, automation or custom deployment, but their pricing units, workflows and claims are not directly equivalent. ([Rossum pricing](https://rossum.ai/pricing/); [Nanonets pricing](https://nanonets.com/pricing); [Veryfi pricing](https://www.veryfi.com/pricing/); [Mindee pricing](https://mindee.com/pricing); [Doxis/Klippa](https://www.klippa.com/en/ocr/)) Treat language counts and “accuracy” marketing as product claims until tested against the required fields and documents.

For open-weight image-to-JSON extraction, plausible single-GPU candidates include Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B, Qwen3-VL 8B, InternVL3.5, and compact document models such as PaddleOCR-VL or Granite-Docling. Qwen2.5-VL explicitly targets structured document outputs and supports dynamic resolution; Qwen3-VL and InternVL report broad OCR/document capabilities. These are model-team claims and general document evaluations, not evidence of accounting-grade invoice extraction. ([Qwen2.5-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [Qwen3-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct); [InternVL3.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B); [PaddleOCR-VL](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL); [Granite-Docling](https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-docling-258M)) For a rough memory starting point, 7B-class BF16 weights alone are around 14–18 GB depending on the actual checkpoint; 4-bit weight-only storage is roughly 3.5–4.5 GB before vision activations, KV cache, unquantized modules, runtime and fragmentation. A 7B 4-bit setup is plausible on 12–16 GB at batch one with controlled image resolution; 24 GB gives more room. These are sizing estimates, not guaranteed requirements; cap image pixels/visual tokens and measure peak memory on actual long receipts and multipage documents. ([HF quantization guidance](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/quantization/bitsandbytes); [Qwen2.5-VL processor guidance](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/model_doc/qwen2_5_vl)) Quantization, image resizing and batching are quality/throughput knobs: benchmark each against exact field and line-item correctness.

Large hosted multimodal APIs remove model hosting and can handle unusual visual semantics quickly. GPT-4.1/4o, Claude, Gemini and hosted specialist OCR such as Mistral OCR 4.1 offer different image-token accounting, context, schema-constrained output, geographic and contractual controls. ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing/); [Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Vertex AI pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing); [Mistral OCR](https://docs.mistral.ai/models/ocr-4-1/)) Structured-output mechanisms constrain response shape, not factual correctness. ([OpenAI Structured Outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs); [Anthropic structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs); [Gemini structured output](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/multimodal/control-generated-output)) Hosted models fit heterogeneous layouts, fast pilots and exception queues; open weights fit data locality, offline control, predictable high-volume use when utilization is high, and model adaptation. A hybrid often wins operationally: OCR/layout for evidence, VLM for difficult semantic mapping, deterministic validation for accounting invariants.

## Benchmarks show capability, not a universal winner

The most relevant public data differ materially in domain, language, labels and scoring. CORD’s released annotated set has 1,000 Indonesian receipts with nested item and total labels; SROIE has 1,000 scanned receipts and separates localization, OCR and a small header-field extraction task. DocILE is more invoice/business-document aligned, with 6.7k labeled documents, 55 field classes and line-item recognition, plus synthetic and unlabeled material. ([CORD](https://github.com/clovaai/cord); [SROIE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10213); [DocILE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)) FUNSD (199 forms) and XFUND (multilingual forms) help test entity linking/layout transfer, not invoice readiness. ([FUNSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13538); [XFUND/LayoutXLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08836)) EPHOIE is potentially relevant to Chinese invoices, but primary release/access details were not confirmed in the notes; verify before relying on it. Dataset access also does not automatically grant commercial image or training rights.

Other widely cited results are complementary diagnostics, not interchangeable scores. DocVQA measures question answering and ANLS grants partial string similarity; OCRBench aggregates several OCR/VQA/KIE tasks; OmniDocBench evaluates PDF parsing; olmOCR-Bench tests document linearization. For example, a model-card DocVQA score, a CORD entity F1, an OmniDocBench parsing score and a commercial vendor’s “accuracy” claim have different targets and protocols. Even results within the same broad benchmark can differ by split, evaluator, OCR access, prompt, rendering, model version and resolution. **Do not describe them as head-to-head unless those conditions match.** ([DocVQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00398); [OCRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07895); [OmniDocBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07626); [olmOCR-Bench](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/tree/main/olmocr/bench))

For deployment, make a blind internal evaluation the decision benchmark. Report normalized exact match for supplier, identifier, dates, currency, subtotal, tax and total; per-field precision/recall/F1 and support; document-level all-critical-fields-correct rate; line-item row matching and cell/association accuracy; arithmetic reconciliation; unsupported-value rate; JSON/schema validity; abstention and review coverage; and p50/p95 latency and total cost. Keep monetary exactness distinct from any justified tolerance, and define safe normalization rules before scoring. Split by supplier/template and preferably time, not just random pages, to avoid near-duplicate leakage. Stratify by language, photo versus born-digital, scan quality, page count, novel vendors, handwriting/stamps and table complexity. Preserve a private hard-case set and report confidence intervals and raw counts.

## Czech účtenky require language-aware controls

Czech is not adequately covered by a generic claim of Latin-script or European-language OCR. Textract’s documented text-language list excludes Czech, and its listed character inventory omits several Czech-specific letters, including č, ď, ě, ň, ř, š, ť, ů and ž. That makes it an unsuitable default without direct validation. ([Textract language/character limits](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/limits-document.html)) EasyOCR offers language-selectable OCR and documents language-specific character sets and dictionaries, but this demonstrates configurable OCR—not Czech invoice/receipt field accuracy. ([EasyOCR](https://github.com/JaidedAI/EasyOCR)) Azure publishes broad invoice language coverage, while Google’s broad general OCR coverage does not prove dedicated Invoice Parser performance for Czech. Confirm language support for the exact processor, API version and target fields, then test actual documents. ([Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/language-support/prebuilt?view=doc-intel-4.0.0); [Google processors](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/processors-list)) The research found no authoritative public Czech účtenky field-level dataset or comparative Czech extraction benchmark.

Keep source strings in Unicode and preserve diacritics; an accent-folded search key can supplement, never replace, the canonical value. A Czech-specific evaluation should include narrow thermal paper, faded and crumpled photos, clipped edges, tiny print, merchant/product names, abbreviations, item wrapping, VAT groupings, discounts and both receipts (účtenky) and invoices (faktury). Normalize number and date formats only after forming and recording a locale hypothesis: Czech values commonly use comma decimals and dates such as `03.04.2025` as day-month-year, but mixed-country invoices demand caution. Preserve raw and normalized values and flag ambiguity. Verify VAT identifiers and IBANs with appropriate structural/checksum and authoritative validation; checksums can detect transcription errors but cannot prove ownership or transaction correctness.

Where available, prefer machine-readable e-invoices (e.g., structured XML/UBL/CII conforming to EN 16931) over visual OCR. EN 16931 is a structured semantic standard; a PDF image of an invoice is not equivalent. ([European Commission eInvoicing](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/display/DIGITAL/eInvoicing)) Tax/legal requirements vary by transaction and exception, so extraction validation is not a substitute for Czech accounting/tax rules review. ([Czech VAT Act 235/2004 Coll.](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2004-235); [EU VAT Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/112/oj))

## Deploy with evidence, validation and selective review

Start with a canonical schema that defines aliases and semantics (especially subtotal, tax, amount due, paid, gratuity, discount and total), optional/missing values, repeated fields, line-item grouping, date/currency representation and raw evidence. Then establish three matched routes where feasible: specialist hosted parser; local OCR/layout plus rules or a fine-tuned tagger; and direct image-to-schema hosted/open VLM. Use the same held-out documents, schema, field definitions and resolution policy. For open adaptation, begin with pretrained weights and a labeled pilot, compare prompting with fine-tuning, and measure learning curves across vendor diversity; LoRA/QLoRA can reduce training-state memory but does not itself guarantee better extraction. Synthetic data can broaden controlled layout/field coverage, but retain substantial real-document tests and avoid synthetic-to-real claims without measurement. ([QLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314); [PEFT quantization](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/main/en/developer_guides/quantization); [Donut/SynthDoG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15664))

Production should preserve page and field provenance: raw OCR text, source box/crop, extracted and normalized value, model/API version, score and validation status. Apply deterministic checks for required fields, date plausibility, currency-aware decimal parsing, duplicate IDs, line extension, tax/total arithmetic and vendor/PO matching. These checks detect inconsistencies but do not prove a visually ambiguous source was read correctly; do not silently overwrite evidence to make arithmetic balance. Confidence values from APIs and VLMs are routing signals, not calibrated probabilities unless validated on representative labels. Route high-impact or contradictory fields to assisted review; auto-accept only after critical-field thresholds and business checks pass. Show reviewers the source crop, raw value, normalized value and failing rule, and record corrections as governed training feedback. ([AWS confidence guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/textract-best-practices.html); [Azure confidence guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/concept/accuracy-confidence?view=doc-intel-4.0.0); [UiPath Validation Station](https://docs.uipath.com/activities/other/latest/document-understanding/validation-station))

| Situation | Practical starting recommendation |
|---|---|
| Low volume, fastest time to value | Trial a dedicated invoice/receipt API on a representative sample, retain its OCR/evidence output and add application-side validation/review. Compare at least one other provider before committing. |
| High volume, stable vendor set | Native PDF text + OCR fallback, vendor-specific rules or a fine-tuned token/layout extractor; route unknown layouts to a general parser and exceptions to review. Monitor drift and template changes. |
| High layout variability / frequent schema changes | Hosted VLM for a pilot or exception lane, with strict nullable schema, cited source evidence, arithmetic checks and review. Compare direct image prompting with OCR-plus-VLM. |
| Privacy-sensitive / offline / EU-controlled | Self-host a permissively licensed OCR/layout model and, if needed, a quantized 3B–7B VLM; pin weights/dependencies, verify each license, restrict logs and storage, and budget GPU operations and security ownership. A cloud region alone does not settle retention or transfer obligations. |
| Czech receipts and invoices | First prove diacritic recognition on local documents. Compare Czech-capable OCR plus a local field model/VLM with shortlisted APIs; avoid assuming Textract or generic language counts cover Czech. Evaluate VAT, comma decimals, date ambiguity, long thermal receipts and line items. |
| Very high accuracy / financial controls | Use evidence-grounded extraction and field-specific calibrated thresholds; require deterministic reconciliation and human confirmation for critical conflicts. No model/API alone guarantees correct financial values. |

For reliability, use asynchronous queues for volume, idempotent job identifiers and bounded retries for transient errors; route unsupported formats/languages to a terminal exception rather than blind retries. Handle multi-page aggregation explicitly—Textract expense analysis treats pages independently and does not preserve cross-page context. ([AWS async processing](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/async.html)) Monitor p50/p95 end-to-end latency, pages per hour, retries, provider quotas, GPU utilization/peak VRAM, cost per accepted record, error slices and reviewer minutes. For self-hosting, include idle utilization, preprocessing, storage, updates, observability and on-call rather than GPU seconds alone.

Invoices and receipts may contain personal data and sensitive commercial information in images, OCR text, structured values and logs. Under GDPR, define purpose and lawful basis, minimize fields, retention and access, use appropriate security, and review processor contracts, subprocessors, regions, deletion and transfer mechanisms. Financial data is not automatically a GDPR special category, but purchases can reveal sensitive facts in context. Open weights do not remove controller obligations, and “open” licenses vary; check the exact checkpoint, code and dataset terms. ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng); [EDPB processor guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-072020-concepts-controller-and-processor-gdpr_en))

## Conclusion

The field’s genuine advance is flexibility: OCR-free models and multimodal LLMs can interpret unfamiliar pages and schemas with far less bespoke template engineering. The operational breakthrough, however, is not a particular model—it is maintaining a verifiable chain from original page to field evidence to normalized value to accounting checks and review. The right architecture is therefore usually selective: deterministic routes for machine-readable and stable documents, specialized extraction for routine throughput, and VLM reasoning for the long tail.

For Czech use, local documents and field definitions are the missing evidence. A modest, carefully sampled corpus split by merchant and time—scored for exact money/date/identifier values, item associations and abstention—will answer the procurement question more reliably than any current generic leaderboard. Recalculate dated prices against actual image usage, document/page mix, utilization and human review; then decide on cost per correct accepted record.
