# Build reliable real-time scene text OCR

For real-time camera OCR without translation or replacement, **start with a specialist detector–recognizer pipeline, not a general VLM**: PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5/v6 or RapidOCR provides the most practical open deployment path, while DB/DB++-style polygon detection and compact CTC/SVTR-family recognition address variable scale, rotation and text geometry. For Czech–English consumer devices, a compact multilingual baseline plus a shared Latin recognizer that preserves Czech diacritics is the most credible starting point; neither language-count claims nor published leaderboard results establish accuracy on local camera footage. Competitions show that incidental capture, curved text, clutter and multilingual scripts are distinct problems, and no public benchmark establishes sustained phone-camera accuracy, latency, power or thermal behavior. The defensible system is therefore a lightly conditioned, asynchronous video pipeline with tracking and selective recognition, validated on held-out Czech/English sequences. Below, **measured** refers only to explicitly described paper/repository benchmark measurements; **vendor/paper claim** identifies source-reported results; **estimate/recommendation** identifies engineering proposals that remain to be tested.

## Specialist OCR pipelines offer the best real-time starting point

Scene OCR has two separable tasks: finding text regions and reading their contents. A two-stage system makes that distinction operationally useful: detector recall and geometry can be debugged independently from crop recognition, and recognizers can be swapped without retraining detection. **PaddleOCR is the strongest turnkey candidate in the reviewed set**, with a maintained detection/recognition pipeline, model tiers and documented deployment routes; its current materials announce PP-OCRv6 tiny/small/medium at 1.5M/7.7M/34.5M parameters and a unified model covering 50 languages, including Latin-script languages. These are project/vendor descriptions, not an independent Czech-camera evaluation. Its README also reports 0.13 s on an A100 for a listed configuration and speedups on OpenVINO/Apple M4; inputs, scope and hardware protocol are not sufficiently normalized for cross-project comparison. ([PaddleOCR](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR); [PP-OCRv6 weights](https://huggingface.co/collections/PaddlePaddle/pp-ocrv6))

**RapidOCR is an inference/deployment layer around converted PaddleOCR models**, rather than a distinct detection architecture. It lists ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO, MNN, Paddle, PyTorch and TensorRT options and maintains native/mobile-oriented project paths, making it a strong option when runtime portability matters; exact model/backend combinations and platform behavior need verification. EasyOCR is a simple Python prototype with CRAFT detection, CRNN recognition, four-corner boxes and confidence values. docTR offers configurable DB/LinkNet/FAST detectors and CRNN/SAR/MASTER/ViTSTR/PARSeq/VIPTR recognizers, with rotated-box support. MMOCR provides the broadest research menu, but its PyTorch/OpenMMLab stack and model/config assembly make it less turnkey. Tesseract is a mature LSTM line-recognition engine, not a complete general scene-text detector; OpenCV exposes DB/EAST detection and CNN-RNN-CTC recognition APIs, but requires assembling the pipeline and selecting compatible weights. ([RapidOCR](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR); [model list](https://rapidai.github.io/RapidOCRDocs/main/model_list/); [EasyOCR](https://github.com/JaidedAI/EasyOCR); [docTR](https://github.com/mindee/doctr); [MMOCR](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmocr); [Tesseract](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesseract); [OpenCV text spotting](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d4/d43/tutorial_dnn_text_spotting.html))

Detector geometry is a first-order choice when text is rotated, perspective-skewed or curved. DB/DB++ produce text-region maps and polygon-friendly geometry; DB++ adds adaptive scale fusion aimed at scale variation. TextSnake represents irregular text through centerlines, radii and orientations. ABCNet/ABCNet v2 uses Bezier curves and BezierAlign for arbitrarily shaped text, integrating recognition into a research-oriented spotter. These methods are better matched to curved boundaries than axis-aligned boxes, but add postprocessing or integration burden. DB’s paper reports 82.8 F-measure and 62 FPS for ResNet-18 on MSRA-TD500; **this is a historical paper measurement on a specific dataset and setup, not a modern mobile live-stream result**. ([DB paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08947); [DBNet++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10304); [TextSnake](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01544); [ABCNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10200); [ABCNet v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03620))

Recognition families also solve different subproblems. CRNN/CTC and compact CNN/sequence recognizers are practical deployment baselines; SVTR uses visual-transformer features without an explicit recurrent decoder. PARSeq is a strong crop recognizer with permutation language modeling and iterative refinement; its repository reports 95.95% exact word accuracy over 7,672 samples in a specified standard-dataset protocol, but that result uses a lowercase-alphanumeric setup and does not establish Czech diacritics or phone latency. PARSeq’s rotation tolerance does not replace detection and crop rectification. ABINet’s language component can help ambiguous text while potentially normalizing unusual strings; SATRN’s 2-D attention targets distorted lines; TrOCR offers pretrained encoder-decoder recognition but is not a complete scene spotter and autoregressive generation can cost more. Compare recognizers on fixed, identical crops and charset, since training data, lexicons, resizing and evaluation protocol materially confound published recognition tables. ([SVTR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00159); [PARSeq](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06966); [ABINet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06495); [SATRN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04396); [TrOCR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10282); [protocol analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906))

## VLMs can read selected text but do not replace exhaustive spotting

General vision-language models are compelling when the user asks a question about a sign or selects a region, but OCRBench and TextVQA measure perception-plus-answering rather than finding every text instance with calibrated boxes and confidence. Model-card scores illustrate the mismatch: Qwen2.5-VL-7B reports OCRBench 864, while MiniCPM-o 2.6 reports 897; the latter notes prompt/chain-of-thought conditions, so these are **model-team claims on a QA-style benchmark, not a controlled head-to-head scene OCR test**. Dynamic resolution and tiling can preserve small text, at higher token, latency and memory cost. ([Qwen2.5-VL-7B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [MiniCPM-o 2.6](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-o-2_6); [OCRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07818))

Among compact promptable models, Florence-2 has explicit `<OCR>` and `<OCR_WITH_REGION>` modes that return text and quadrilateral coordinates; GOT-OCR2.0 (580M parameters) supports OCR and user-directed region/crop workflows. Both merit targeted experiments, but the cited material does not establish exhaustive scene-text recall, Czech/English accuracy or sustained camera throughput against specialist OCR. Qwen/InternVL/MiniCPM and similar larger VLMs suit contextual reading or exception handling more than continuous whole-frame transcription. A useful hybrid is conventional detection and recognition for coverage, then a VLM only for a selected ambiguous crop or user question; this keeps omissions, false detections, and latency measurable. ([Florence-2](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Florence-2-large); [GOT-OCR2.0](https://github.com/Ucas-HaoranWei/GOT-OCR2.0); [InternVL3-8B](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3-8B))

The practical distinction is output contract. VLMs often generate a fluent answer, omit background text, merge reading order, or produce prompted rather than exhaustive coordinates; conventional spotters expose region-level recall and transcripts. OCRBench, DocVQA and document parsing benchmarks do not substitute for scene-text detection/recognition metrics. **For a live overlay or searchable scene transcript, use VLMs as an optional second opinion, not the primary detector**, unless a target-device test demonstrates acceptable coverage and stable latency. This is a task-based recommendation, not a claim that a VLM cannot read an individual word. ([OCRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07818); [Florence-2 model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Florence-2-large); [Qwen2.5-VL model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct))

## Competitions isolate hard cases but cannot certify live-camera performance

ICDAR Robust Reading competitions provide useful, protocol-specific evidence. ICDAR 2015 “Incidental Scene Text” deliberately includes poorly framed Google Glass captures, small and rotated text, perspective distortion and blur, making it more relevant to handheld use than carefully framed text sets. ICDAR 2013 and later RRC editions define distinct tasks and metrics; “ICDAR winner” is not meaningful without naming year, track, split, lexicon condition and rules. The reviewed evidence did not verify a single current 2026 ranking across the relevant tasks, so this survey does not assert one. ([ICDAR 2015 paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2015.7298693); [RRC portal](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/); [ICDAR 2013 paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2013.11))

Dataset choice should follow the failure mode: COCO-Text and TextOCR cover clutter and small text; Total-Text, CTW1500 and ArT emphasize curved/arbitrary shapes; ICDAR MLT adds script diversity; TextZoom supports paired real-camera low/high-resolution recognition analysis. These datasets are complementary and mostly still images. They do not jointly reproduce motion trajectories, rolling shutter, autofocus hunting, exposure changes, temporal flicker, battery use or thermals. Report detection precision/recall/F-score or AP under the dataset’s matching rules, plus recognition word exact match/CER/normalized edit distance; end-to-end spotting must count both localization and transcription failure. ([COCO-Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07140); [TextOCR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12462); [Total-Text](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2017.250); [CTW1500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02170); [ArT](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Chng_Arbitrary-Shaped_Text_Detection_With_Adaptive_Scale_Embedding_CVPR_2019_paper.html); [ICDAR MLT](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2017.280); [TextZoom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03341))

Leaderboard values cannot be treated as a universal OCR percentage. Differences in annotation unit, scripts, test resizing, external/synthetic training data, lexicons, test-time augmentation, polygon matching and recognition vocabulary can reverse rankings. VLM answer scores measure a different construct again. Even true published speed results are usually a model call or a particular paper inference configuration, not camera-to-overlay age. A **measured** PARSeq repository microbenchmark reports 14.87 ms median at batch one for a 32×128 input and three non-autoregressive refinement iterations, but the cited result does not identify the device and excludes camera, detection and app overhead. Paddle’s A100 and M4 values are vendor-reported configurations. Neither predicts sustained phone performance. ([comparison analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906); [PARSeq benchmark](https://github.com/baudm/parseq); [PaddleOCR](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR))

## Capture-aware preprocessing and temporal design matter as much as models

The best default is inexpensive, selective conditioning. Keep the original crop; detect text, preserve quadrilateral corners, rectify rotation/perspective, then test mild grayscale or local contrast enhancement and denoising. CLAHE and adaptive thresholding are available OpenCV operations, but aggressive binarization or morphology can erase thin strokes or merge adjacent glyphs. Glare and clipped highlights are principally capture problems: saturated character pixels cannot be reliably reconstructed, so recapture or abstention is safer than confident restoration. ([OpenCV CLAHE](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d6/db6/classcv_1_1CLAHE.html); [adaptive thresholding](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d7/d4d/tutorial_py_thresholding.html))

For small text, crop before enlarging. Text-specific super-resolution has stronger OCR evidence than generic image restoration: TSRN authors report relative accuracy improvements of over 13% for CRNN and nearly 9% for ASTER/MORAN over synthetic-SR baselines on TextZoom. This is a **paper-reported benchmark result**, not a guaranteed gain for another camera or recognizer. Restormer, SwinIR and Real-ESRGAN report image restoration capability, not OCR accuracy; deblurring/SR can invent plausible strokes. Route enhancement only to uncertain small/blurred crops, run OCR on both original and enhanced versions, preserve both hypotheses, and abstain or request a better frame when they disagree. ([TextZoom/TSRN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03341); [Restormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09881); [SwinIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10257); [Real-ESRGAN](https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN))

Live OCR should be asynchronous and rate-limited rather than run on every preview frame. **Recommended design (estimate, not measured standard):** keep capture and preview responsive; use a bounded latest-frame queue that drops stale frames; detect periodically or on meaningful scene/motion change; track regions between detector passes; recognize only new or changed crops; stabilize text over a short temporal window. An initial 3–8 detection passes per second is a tuning hypothesis, not a published device result. Carry transforms through sensor orientation, mirroring, resize/letterbox, model coordinates and display coordinates; test all orientations and aspect ratios. Target roughly 200–400 ms to a stable result as an initial UX objective, then measure on actual devices. ([ONNX Runtime Mobile](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/get-started/with-mobile.html); [CameraX latest-frame guidance](https://developer.android.com/media/camera/camerax/analyze))

On Apple platforms, Core ML or ONNX Runtime’s CoreML execution provider is a natural route; static shape buckets can help, and operator coverage determines whether work actually reaches GPU/Neural Engine. On Android, compare LiteRT/TFLite delegates, NNAPI and device-specific accelerators such as Qualcomm QNN, inspecting partition/fallback logs; an accelerator API does not guarantee the graph runs fully on an NPU. ONNX Runtime Mobile supports both ecosystems. TensorRT is relevant for NVIDIA GPU/Jetson, not ordinary phones. FP16 is a sensible first optimization where supported; INT8 requires representative calibration and accuracy checks, especially for diacritics, small glyphs and low contrast. ([ORT CoreML EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/CoreML-ExecutionProvider.html); [ORT NNAPI EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/NNAPI-ExecutionProvider.html); [ORT TensorRT EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/TensorRT-ExecutionProvider.html); [ORT quantization](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/performance/model-optimizations/quantization.html))

## Czech–English recommendation, comparison matrix and test plan

For Czech-English consumer-device use, begin with **PP-OCRv6 tiny/small through PaddleOCR or RapidOCR**, depending on the desired runtime, alongside a Czech-labelled evaluation of its exact recognizer. Its project claims Latin-script coverage but does not publish the needed per-condition Czech-English result. In parallel, establish a controllable reference with a compact DB-family detector, perspective crop rectification and a CTC recognizer trained or fine-tuned with a shared Czech-English charset. Include `á č ď é ě í ň ó ř š ť ú ů ý ž` in both cases, plus punctuation, digits and mixed-language proper names; do not accent-fold canonical output. Test PARSeq-Tiny on the same fixed crops as an accuracy-oriented alternative. If curvature is a major product case, add DB++ or an ABCNet-family spotter; if only one selected label needs interpretation, evaluate Florence-2 region OCR or a VLM as a selective fallback.

| Candidate | What it contributes | Fit for harsh camera OCR | Deployment / evidence | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5/v6 | Integrated detector, orientation and recognizer; multiple size/language choices | Best turnkey baseline; polygon geometry and scale must be verified per chosen model | Maintained toolchain; CPU/GPU and documented ONNX/OpenVINO/TensorRT paths; reported scores/speeds are vendor claims | No independent Czech-English live-camera result; per-version model/backend parity varies |
| RapidOCR | Paddle-derived pipeline with portable converted model runtimes | Same underlying model-family strengths; useful for native/mobile integration | ONNX Runtime, MNN, OpenVINO, TensorRT and mobile/native project routes documented | Conversion/backend support is version-specific; not a new model family |
| DB/DB++ + CTC/SVTR recognizer | Modular detection and recognition; polygon-oriented DB geometry | Strong candidate for multi-scale, rotated text; DB++ targets scale fusion | Flexible research/deployment assembly; DB historical paper result is dataset-specific | More integration and export work; no comparable device benchmark |
| EasyOCR | CRAFT + CRNN pipeline with boxes/confidence | Accessible prototype and broad-script baseline | Python/PyTorch; Reader can be reused | No documented native streaming/mobile stack or controlled speed result |
| docTR / MMOCR | Configurable document/scene model families | Useful for experimentation; MMOCR includes curved-text and spotting methods | Python research/toolkit ecosystems | Dependency/configuration burden; not turnkey phone camera SDKs |
| PARSeq | Crop recognizer with strong reported standard-set recognition | Alternative for rotated or difficult crops after detection/rectification | Checkpoints, charset controls and export guidance; 14.87 ms reported microbenchmark lacks identified device | Recognizer only; published setup does not establish Czech diacritics or mobile speed |
| Florence-2 / GOT-OCR2.0 | Promptable OCR, including region/crop modes | Worth testing for selected regions and flexible extraction | Florence-2 0.23B/0.77B; GOT 580M; weight size is not latency | No comparable exhaustive word-recall/live-camera benchmark; licensing differs by artifact/data |
| Qwen/InternVL/MiniCPM VLMs | Contextual image QA and reading | Useful for user-selected text or ambiguity resolution | 7–9B BF16 weight arithmetic suggests roughly 16–20 GB before runtime overhead; quantization changes fit | QA benchmark scores do not measure exhaustive spotting; image tokens add variable cost |
| Tesseract / OpenCV DNN | Mature recognizer or lower-level inference primitives | Baseline/embedded component when crop quality and engineering control are available | Tesseract C++ / OpenCV Apache-2.0 projects | Tesseract lacks learned scene detector; OpenCV requires compatible model assembly and pretrained language limitations |

The shortlist above is a synthesis, not a ranked head-to-head benchmark. “Harsh conditions” should be resolved into measurable strata: text pixel height, font/stylization, angle/perspective, curvature, motion blur, defocus, glare, low light/noise, compression, occlusion, script and device. No model in the reviewed evidence is shown to dominate all of these simultaneously.

**Testing plan (proposed):** assemble annotated stills and short videos from storefronts, transport, menus, packaging and screens, with genuine Czech diacritics, English and code-switching. Capture across devices, sessions and locations; split by source/session, never adjacent frames, to avoid leakage. Supplement—but do not replace the private test—with ICDAR 2015 for incidental capture, TextOCR/COCO-Text for clutter, Total-Text/CTW1500 for curved shapes, MLT for script diversity and TextZoom for low-resolution diagnostics. Record missed, split, merged and false text instances.

Run the same held-out sequences through (1) official PaddleOCR/RapidOCR small-tier defaults, (2) compact DB-family + CTC reference, (3) PARSeq on the exact same detected crops, and (4) one curve-aware or VLM alternative only if its use case matters. Compare FP32/FP16 first and INT8 only with representative calibration. Report detection precision/recall/F-score under a declared matching convention; word exact match, normalized edit distance and CER; end-to-end transcription including detector misses; Czech-diacritic and mixed-language slices; and confidence/abstention behavior. For temporal use, also report time-to-first-correct and stable read, overlay age, duplicate/flicker rate and correction rate.

Lock exact checkpoint hashes, model/export versions, input sizes, thresholds, runtime/provider, OS, device, camera mode and precision. Warm up, then run 5–10 minute sustained camera sessions on a representative mid-tier and flagship iOS and Android device; record p50/p95 frame-to-stable-text latency, detector cadence, throughput, dropped frames, RAM, cold start, energy/power and thermals. Verify accelerator partitioning rather than assuming the selected provider owns inference. An initial quality guardrail—**estimate**—could limit quantization loss to 1 percentage point overall and 2 points on the Czech-diacritic slice; revise it to product risk before measuring. No public source supplies a universal pass threshold.

Licensing must be checked artifact by artifact. PaddleOCR, RapidOCR, EasyOCR, MMOCR, docTR, Tesseract and OpenCV list Apache-2.0 at project level, but this does not automatically license every checkpoint, dependency or training dataset. RapidOCR documents upstream model provenance; research weights and community quantizations need their own terms. GOT’s code is Apache-2.0 while its repository identifies training data under CC BY-NC 4.0; verify the checkpoint separately. VLM cards may carry distinct terms from code and components. Record code, weights, tokenizer/charset, conversion and dataset terms before redistribution or commercial use. ([PaddleOCR](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR); [RapidOCR model licenses](https://github.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR/blob/main/MODEL_LICENSES.md); [GOT-OCR2.0](https://github.com/Ucas-HaoranWei/GOT-OCR2.0))

## Conclusion

The central engineering choice is not a leaderboard winner but whether the pipeline preserves enough pixels and geometry to make every later stage auditable. A small detector-recognizer, temporal tracking and crop-level selective enhancement is the best-supported path to responsive consumer OCR; VLMs are most valuable when the task changes from “read everything” to “interpret this selected text.”

For Czech-English, a release’s language count is a hypothesis, not evidence. The decisive evidence will be a device-held-out video set that exposes diacritic errors, tiny text and motion failures while measuring stable-result latency and thermal behavior together. Build that evaluation early; it will identify whether the next investment belongs in capture control, detector geometry, Czech recognition data or runtime optimization.
