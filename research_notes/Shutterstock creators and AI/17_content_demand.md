# Stock content demand: categories, buyer trends, and authenticity

**Research date: September 27, 2026.** The scope is commercial stock photography, video, and illustration. Trend reports are directional/editorial signals, not audited marketplace sales data; Shutterstock’s public trend pages were blocked from direct retrieval in this session, so named trend examples should be checked against the linked originals before quoting verbatim.

## What buyers seek and current demand categories

### Takeaway
Commercial buyers need adaptable, usable assets that communicate a recognizable story quickly: people and relationships, work and technology, health and wellbeing, food and travel, and topical culture. Demand is not just for a subject: copy space, flexible crops, useful formats, rights clarity, and a believable moment determine whether an asset works in a campaign.

### Cited Findings
- Shutterstock markets photos, vectors, illustrations, and video as searchable/licensable assets, and its contributor program explicitly accepts these formats; its product taxonomy is evidence of categories supported by the marketplace, not a public ranking of sales — [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/); [Shutterstock Contributor](https://submit.shutterstock.com/).
- Shutterstock’s annual Creative Trends material presents trends as an interpretation of search data and cultural signals. Its 2025 trend coverage includes playful/surreal aesthetics and tactile, “gummy” visual treatments, alongside beauty and design cues; use it as a near-term creative brief, not a guarantee of buyer purchases — [Shutterstock 2025 Creative Trends](https://www.shutterstock.com/blog/2025-creative-trends).
- Shutterstock’s 2024 trend outlook also foregrounded high-impact, emotionally legible aesthetics and internet-born cultural influence, suggesting that the report’s annual trend layer changes faster than the basic need for usable commercial imagery — [Shutterstock 2024 Creative Trends](https://www.shutterstock.com/blog/2024-creative-trends).
- Adobe’s annual Creative Trends reports are an independent-adjacent competitor perspective (also a stock supplier); its 2025 outlook emphasizes expressive, emotionally resonant creative and immersive/experimental visual approaches. These are forecast themes rather than measured stock-asset transactions — [Adobe 2025 Creative Trends](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2025/02/04/adobe-creative-trends-2025).
- Shutterstock’s public product pages distinguish still imagery, vectors, and footage, and promote video in HD/4K; a buyer’s format need therefore spans static campaign art, scalable graphic elements, and moving footage — [Shutterstock Photos](https://www.shutterstock.com/photos); [Shutterstock Video](https://www.shutterstock.com/video).

### Inferences
- Evergreen commercial categories remain people in everyday contexts (family, friendship, community, work, learning), healthcare and wellness, food, home/lifestyle, travel, nature, and business/technology. These work across industries and recurring campaign calendars; this is a practical synthesis of stock-marketplace use cases, not a Shutterstock-published ranked list.
- The most reusable people assets show an action or relationship with room for design adaptation: a wide establishing frame, clean background/copy space, multiple orientations, authentic expressions, and natural interaction. For video, include short, stable, well-framed clips with usable beginnings/endings and variations; for illustration, provide coherent series, editable vectors, and clear visual metaphors.
- Near-term creative briefs can layer currently salient treatments (surreal play, tactile materials, vivid color, beauty/aura effects, nostalgia or internet aesthetics) onto evergreen subjects. This balances short-lived style demand against broader utility.
- Buyers likely seek assets that can be deployed in multi-channel campaigns. Vertical/mobile crops, horizontal hero compositions, square/social variants, and footage that can be cut into short-form edits increase practical fit; these are workflow inferences, not an independently measured buyer preference statistic.

### Gaps
- No accessible, public Shutterstock dataset was located giving current downloads, revenue, or search-conversion rankings by subject, medium, region, or customer type. The trend reports’ search signals should not be represented as sales data.
- Public reports do not establish a universal category ranking across stills, clips, and illustrations; demand varies by buyer segment, geography, season, license, and campaign.

## Evergreen versus emerging topics

### Takeaway
Evergreen subjects solve repeated communication needs; emerging themes provide freshness and cultural relevance. A durable contributor strategy is to create technically strong, differentiated versions of recurring subjects, then selectively test new visual treatments and timely concepts rather than betting a portfolio on a single annual trend.

### Cited Findings
- Shutterstock’s yearly reports explicitly frame creative trends as a changing annual outlook. The 2025 report’s playful surrealism/tactile design signals are examples of time-sensitive aesthetic directions — [Shutterstock 2025 Creative Trends](https://www.shutterstock.com/blog/2025-creative-trends).
- Adobe also publishes annual trend forecasts, reinforcing that major creative platforms treat visual style and cultural mood as moving signals for the coming year, rather than permanent categories — [Adobe 2025 Creative Trends](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2025/02/04/adobe-creative-trends-2025).
- Stock platforms maintain broad catalogues spanning everyday commercial subjects and formats; Shutterstock’s marketplace and contributor pages document the continuing availability of photos, vectors/illustrations, and footage, though not their relative performance — [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/); [Shutterstock Contributor](https://submit.shutterstock.com/).

### Inferences
- **Evergreen:** authentic human interaction; varied family structures and age groups; small businesses and different kinds of work; education; physical and mental health; food preparation/eating; home life; travel; environment and weather; everyday technology. Specific briefs benefit from contemporary details and regional accuracy instead of generic “business handshake” staging.
- **Emerging/volatile:** generative-AI-adjacent workflows and human/AI collaboration; playful surrealism; tactile 3D/material aesthetics; beauty and identity experimentation; creator culture; new platform formats; emerging technologies and climate adaptation. These require frequent updating and careful distinction between real documentary depiction and conceptual illustration.
- Treat trend names as discovery prompts. Build a subject-led series that remains useful if the visual style falls out of fashion; offer alternate treatments, colorways, crops, and formats where production economics allow.

### Gaps
- Public annual trend forecasts do not reveal how long a trend remains commercially productive or the return on production for an individual contributor.
- Reliable, comparable longitudinal data on stock searches/downloads by trend and region was not available in the reviewed public sources.

## Authenticity, representation, and underrepresented imagery

### Takeaway
Buyers increasingly need imagery that feels credible and depicts a broader range of people and lived experience. Representation is strongest when it is specific, dignified, and integrated into ordinary life—not reduced to a token identity cue or a stereotyped “diversity” scene.

### Cited Findings
- Getty Images’ VisualGPS is its research and insight platform on visual communication and consumer/creative expectations; it is a useful independent-marketplace counterpart to Shutterstock’s trend forecasting, though Getty is also a stock agency with a commercial interest — [Getty Images VisualGPS](https://www.gettyimages.com/visualgps).
- Getty has published research and guidance around authentic representation, inclusion, and real-life imagery through VisualGPS and its creative insights. The precise population, question wording, and year differ across studies; avoid turning an isolated survey result into a universal buyer preference — [Getty Images Creative Insights](https://www.gettyimages.com/creative-insights); [VisualGPS](https://www.gettyimages.com/visualgps).
- The Association of Photographers and industry bodies have highlighted the importance of authentic, diverse representation in commercial imagery; this is qualitative industry guidance rather than evidence of stock-market sales rankings — [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/).

### Inferences
- Shoot underrepresented people as full protagonists across ordinary contexts—at work, at home, in leisure, leadership, care, education, and community—rather than limiting them to identity-specific campaigns. Include visible and non-visible disability, varied body types, ages, skin tones, gender expression, family forms, and socioeconomic contexts where appropriate and consented.
- Specificity improves credibility: show real environments, natural interactions, contemporary clothing/technology, and culturally accurate details. Avoid flattening distinct communities into interchangeable visual shorthand.
- Where depicting health, disability, religion, or other sensitive identity, prioritize participant agency, informed releases, respectful direction, and accurate context. Label conceptual/staged scenes honestly; do not imply documentary truth where the image is constructed.
- For footage, capture a range of people and interactions with adequate releases and context; for illustration, create inclusive character systems with consistent proportions, details, and non-stereotyped roles. Consider whether a set gives buyers genuine combinations, rather than one isolated “diversity” asset.

### Gaps
- I could not verify a current, specific VisualGPS survey percentage through direct source retrieval in this session; no numeric consumer-demand claim is included.
- Public evidence does not show a single universal definition of “authentic” or quantify how authenticity affects licensing conversion across stock agencies.

## Source-quality note

Shutterstock and Adobe reports are primary sources for what those companies are forecasting and often draw on platform search/creative signals, but both have commercial interests. Getty VisualGPS supplies a useful second agency perspective, not a neutral census. The findings above separate published forecasts and product offerings from practical inferences; market-share, sales, and exact category-rank claims require proprietary or independently audited data.
