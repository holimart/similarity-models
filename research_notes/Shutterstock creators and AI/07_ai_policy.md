# Shutterstock AI-generated content policies

**Research checked:** 27 September 2026. Policy pages can change; the dates below are the publication/update dates displayed by Shutterstock when available. This is a source-based policy summary, not legal advice.

## Upload eligibility, contributor declarations, and restrictions

### Takeaway

Shutterstock’s marketplace upload policy has generally excluded contributor-submitted AI-generated content, despite the company operating its own AI generation products and licensing content for model training. The exact current contributor help text could not be retrieved in this research session, so verify the live contributor portal before relying on the historical rule or any edge case involving AI-assisted editing.

### Cited Findings

- Shutterstock’s contributor help center is the primary location for submission and review rules. Its pages are currently presented through an Intercom help center; a retrieved 2025 article directs submitters to the submission guidelines and support center, but was not itself an AI policy. [Contributor Help Center](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026); [File Integrity help article](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594615-why-was-my-content-rejected-for-file-integrity) (updated 17 Jul 2025).
- Shutterstock’s live public homepage currently describes its collection as “human-made assets,” while also advertising its own AI tools. This establishes a distinction in its product positioning, but does not by itself specify the full contributor submission rule. [Shutterstock homepage](https://www.shutterstock.com/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026; undated page).
- A direct attempt to retrieve a presumed AI help-article URL resolved to an unrelated help article, and Shutterstock’s blog and license pages returned 403 in this research environment. Consequently, exact current prohibition language, exceptions, and treatment of partly AI-assisted or materially edited work could not be confirmed from accessible first-party text.
- The official contributor support center recommends meeting applicable technical and submission requirements and directs contributors to contact support where rejection reasons need clarification. [Contributor support](https://www.shutterstock.com/contributorsupport?l=en) (accessed 27 Sep 2026).

### Inferences

- The public positioning suggests Shutterstock distinguishes between assets created and uploaded by contributors and AI features/products offered to customers. That is not proof of the precise scope of contributor restrictions; do not infer that use of any AI tool in a production workflow is automatically prohibited or permitted.
- Contributors should disclose the use of AI when the submission interface asks for it and seek written support clarification for mixed human/AI workflows, since the currently retrievable sources did not establish a universal declaration mechanism.

### Gaps

- Could not verify the current operative contributor rule on AI-generated submissions, whether the portal includes an AI-specific checkbox or label, or the treatment of AI-assisted retouching, compositing, upscaling, vectorization, and post-generation human editing.
- Could not substantiate current special restrictions for depicting real people, public figures, trademarks, copyrighted characters, or realistic events in AI-generated uploads from accessible first-party policy text.

## Licensing, synthetic data, and labels

### Takeaway

Shutterstock has publicly participated in generative-AI licensing and data partnerships, which is separate from accepting AI-generated marketplace submissions. This research could not retrieve the live terms needed to state the exact customer license granted to AI-generated output or the current labeling/metadata requirements for synthetic material.

### Cited Findings

- Shutterstock’s public homepage presents both a “human-made” stock collection and AI tools, demonstrating these are distinct offerings on the current site. It does not set out license terms in the retrieved homepage content. [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026).
- The Shutterstock contributor help center provides the official policy and legal navigation, including contributor terms, releases, and submission guidance. [Contributor Help Center](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026); [Shutterstock contributor legal center](https://submit.shutterstock.com/legal?language=en) (accessed 27 Sep 2026).
- Shutterstock’s standard licensing page was not retrievable (403) in this research session; no current clause concerning generative-AI outputs, downstream model training, or synthetic-data labeling could therefore be quoted or verified. [Licensing page](https://www.shutterstock.com/license) (accessed 27 Sep 2026; retrieval blocked).
- No accessible official source established whether generated assets are specifically marked “AI-generated,” carry C2PA/content credentials, or receive another customer-facing synthetic-content label. [Shutterstock homepage](https://www.shutterstock.com/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026); [Contributor Help Center](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026).

### Inferences

- Do not conflate Shutterstock licensing its library/data to AI developers with a contributor’s right to upload generated images or with a buyer’s right to use output from Shutterstock’s generative tools; these are different transactions and policy layers.
- “Human-made” homepage marketing is not itself a complete legal definition of synthetic content and should not be relied on to determine the labeling status of every asset.

### Gaps

- Current terms and conditions for licensing Shutterstock AI-generated output, including commercial use, indemnity, identifiable-person restrictions, and prohibited uses.
- Whether synthetic data, AI training data, or generated outputs have distinct licensing terms or labeling obligations.
- Current contributor compensation, opt-in/opt-out, or data-use mechanics associated with any generative AI training arrangement.

## Policy changes and ambiguities

### Takeaway

The company’s public-facing posture combines marketplace stock described as human-made with company-developed AI generation and AI-related data licensing. Because the underlying dated announcements and current legal/help pages were inaccessible or not recovered, a precise chronology and claims about subsequent policy reversals cannot responsibly be stated from this session’s evidence.

### Cited Findings

- The current Shutterstock homepage simultaneously advertises a curated collection of “human-made assets” and AI tools, indicating coexistence of human-produced stock and company AI products. [Shutterstock homepage](https://www.shutterstock.com/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026; undated).
- The official contributor help center is the relevant primary source for active submission guidance, but its accessible landing page did not expose AI-policy text. [Contributor Help Center](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/) (accessed 27 Sep 2026).
- Shutterstock’s contributor support article on file integrity is dated 17 July 2025 and shows that help-center guidance is updated over time; it does not itself address AI. [Why was my content rejected for File Integrity?](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594615-why-was-my-content-rejected-for-file-integrity) (17 Jul 2025).

### Inferences

- Statements about Shutterstock “allowing AI” can refer to its customer-facing generator or its licensing/data partnerships, and should not be treated as evidence that contributor-generated AI images are accepted for stock submission.
- The divergence between available marketing statements and unavailable detailed terms is a material ambiguity: the contributor portal’s live rule and the applicable license at the time of upload/purchase control.

### Gaps

- Exact dates and wording for Shutterstock’s initial AI submission ban, subsequent updates, and any present-day exceptions could not be established using retrievable first-party documents in this session.
- Whether the current policy varies by media type (still image, vector, footage, editorial) or submission channel remains unverified.
- Official URLs should be rechecked directly: Shutterstock blog pages returned HTTP 403, an investor-relations request timed out, and searching the help center did not surface the AI policy article.
