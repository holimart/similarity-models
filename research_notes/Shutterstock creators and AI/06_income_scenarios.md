# Shutterstock creator income scenarios

## What official royalty rates mean in practice

Shutterstock's current earnings schedule lists six image tiers: 15% for licenses 1–100 in the calendar year, 20% for 101–250, 25% for 251–500, 30% for 501–2,500, 35% for 2,501–25,000, and 40% above 25,000. Video has separate thresholds (15% up to 10, 20% for 11–50, 25% for 51–250, 30% for 251–5,000, 35% for 5,001–25,000, and 40% above). Levels reset to level 1 each January; image and video progress independently. [Official earnings schedule](https://submit.shutterstock.com/payouts); [Shutterstock contributor pay explanation](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594604-how-much-will-i-be-paid-as-a-contributor-to-shutterstock)

These percentages are not percentages of a fixed public list price or a creator's gross sales. Shutterstock says commission is a share of the price paid/received for that particular license, and the customer price varies with plan, license type, asset allotment, and individual/team use. For subscriptions and packs, the calculation can use the per-asset price assuming full use of the plan. [How much will I earn per download?](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594609-how-much-will-i-earn-per-download); [Contributor earnings structure FAQ](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594653-contributor-earnings-structure-faq)

## Illustrative scenarios (images only)

The scenarios below make the uncertain inputs visible. They are examples, not reported averages, forecasts, or promises. Assume a single image portfolio, 12 months of licensing, and all stated licenses qualify for Shutterstock's marketplace image tiers. For arithmetic, use a hypothetical **$1.00 average contributor royalty per licensed image download**. This is a modeling convenience, not a published Shutterstock standard rate: actual per-download amounts vary and may be lower or higher depending on the plan, license, customer price, and level. The $1.00 average already represents the contributor's royalty, so do not multiply these scenario results by the tier percentage again.

| Case | Licenses during calendar year | Assumed average creator royalty per license | Gross royalty math | Illustrative gross royalties |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low-volume example | 20 | $1.00 | 20 × $1.00 | $20 |
| Modest-volume example | 120 | $1.00 | 120 × $1.00 | $120 |
| Strong-volume example | 600 | $1.00 | 600 × $1.00 | $600 |
| Very high-volume example | 3,000 | $1.00 | 3,000 × $1.00 | $3,000 |

These totals use a constant average royalty for transparency; they do **not** model the tier ladder license-by-license, because the tier is a percentage of varying customer prices. If one instead wants to calculate from assumed customer receipts, use `sum(each eligible license's customer price received × the applicable royalty percentage)`. For a simplified constant-price illustration only, suppose every qualifying download generated $4 of price received by Shutterstock and every download were paid at one specified tier: at 15%, royalty would be $0.60 per license; at 25%, $1.00; at 35%, $1.40; at 40%, $1.60. That simplification is not a claim that Shutterstock pays the same amount per download or that every license is sold at $4.

### A simple sensitivity range

Holding the assumed license counts fixed, changing only average contributor royalty shows why downloads alone do not determine earnings:

| Annual licenses | At $0.25 average | At $1.00 average | At $2.00 average |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | $5 | $20 | $40 |
| 120 | $30 | $120 | $240 |
| 600 | $150 | $600 | $1,200 |
| 3,000 | $750 | $3,000 | $6,000 |

The $0.25/$1/$2 amounts are analyst-selected scenarios, not Shutterstock rate quotations. Substitute actual account earnings divided by qualifying license counts for a creator-specific historical average; past performance still cannot guarantee future licensing.

## Gross royalties, costs, withholding, and taxes

“Gross royalties” here means modeled contributor royalties credited before the creator's own production/business costs and personal income taxes. Net economic income is not inferable from the platform royalty calculation alone:

`net after business costs and tax = gross royalties − creator's attributable costs − applicable taxes/withholding`

For example, from the $600 gross scenario, if a creator independently budgets $150 in annual expenses and models a 20% effective tax on the remaining $450, the illustrative after-cost, after-tax amount is `$600 − $150 − ($450 × 20%) = $360`. Both the $150 expenses and 20% tax rate are purely illustrative assumptions, not typical costs or tax advice. Actual costs, deductible expenses, tax residence, entity status, treaty eligibility, and rates vary. Shutterstock's terms say non-U.S. contributors may be subject to U.S. withholding on U.S.-source income and may qualify for treaty reductions/exemptions; contributor tax forms and jurisdiction-specific advice matter. [Contributor Terms of Service, compensation and taxes](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594630-contributor-terms-of-service-2023-update)

Payment timing also differs from earned amount: Shutterstock's terms provide a $25 minimum payout per accounting period, with balances below it rolled forward (subject to account and payment conditions). Thus a low annual modeled royalty can be earned but not necessarily paid out in that period. The terms also allow royalty adjustments for refunds/chargebacks and specified deductions. [Contributor Terms of Service](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594630-contributor-terms-of-service-2023-update)

## Why outcomes are highly skewed and income is not guaranteed

1. **A portfolio does not earn uniformly.** Downloads depend on customers finding and licensing particular assets; the scenario's volume assumptions are inputs, not a predictable consequence of uploading a given number of files. Shutterstock explains earnings depend on the plans and licenses customers choose and the asset's level at the time of purchase. [How much will I earn per download?](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594609-how-much-will-i-earn-per-download)
2. **Both demand and value vary.** A small number of standout, relevant assets could account for a large share of a portfolio's licenses, while many items might receive none. This is a plausible portfolio-skew mechanism, not a measured claim about the exact distribution of Shutterstock contributor earnings; the company does not publish a contributor-wide earnings distribution in the cited schedule.
3. **Tier thresholds reset annually.** A creator's percentage can fall back to 15% at the start of a new calendar year, then rise only as new qualifying marketplace licenses accrue. Each license's underlying customer price still varies, so the tier alone does not determine dollar earnings. [Official earnings schedule](https://submit.shutterstock.com/payouts)
4. **Licensing is contingent.** Shutterstock's terms say it has the right, but not the obligation, to license submitted content and may refuse/remove content. No minimum number of licenses or royalty income is promised. [Contributor Terms of Service](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594630-contributor-terms-of-service-2023-update)

Consequently, income should be treated as uncertain and potentially intermittent. “Up to 40%” describes the top royalty percentage tier, not a typical effective rate, guaranteed earnings, or 40% of an assumed retail price. The scenarios do not estimate the probability that a creator reaches any license volume; Shutterstock does not provide enough public cohort/distribution data in the cited sources to do that credibly.

## Inputs, assumptions, and limitations

- **Official inputs:** image and video tier percentages, annual tier resets, variable plan/license pricing, $25 payout threshold, and tax-form/withholding provisions, linked inline above.
- **Modeled inputs:** annual image license counts of 20, 120, 600, and 3,000; average creator royalties of $0.25, $1.00, or $2.00 per license; and the separate $4 customer-price illustration. All are transparent assumptions, not Shutterstock-reported averages.
- **Scope:** image marketplace licenses only. The models exclude video, referral income, data-license Contributor Fund payments, invited Unlimited program revenue sharing, currency conversion, and irregular custom/API deals. Shutterstock lists data and Unlimited as separate earnings mechanisms, and those downloads do not count toward marketplace image/video level thresholds. [Official earnings schedule](https://submit.shutterstock.com/payouts)
- **No representative distribution data:** the cited official sources explain rates and factors but do not disclose median creator income, the share of contributors earning zero, or a reliable license-volume distribution. Do not infer a “typical” income from these examples.
