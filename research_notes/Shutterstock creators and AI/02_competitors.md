# Global stock-content competitors (current as of 2026)

## What market-share evidence is available?

### Takeaway
There is no credible, apples-to-apples public global market-share table for stock imagery, video, editorial, and adjacent licensing in the sources reviewed. Shutterstock, Adobe Stock, and Getty disclose corporate or segment financials that do not map cleanly to comparable stock-only revenue; library size, search visibility, and traffic should therefore be treated as scale indicators, not market share.

### Cited Findings
- Shutterstock's investor-relations site publishes annual reports and financial results, but its reported business spans content, data licensing, and other offerings; consolidated revenue is not a direct measure of stock-content share. [Shutterstock annual reports](https://investor.shutterstock.com/financials/annual-reports-and-proxy/default.aspx)
- Getty Images is a public company with investor disclosures, but Getty Images, iStock, and Unsplash sit in the same brand portfolio, so its corporate metrics do not provide a directly comparable stock-only share measure. [Getty Images investor relations](https://investors.gettyimages.com/); [Getty Images About Us](https://www.gettyimages.com/about-us)
- Adobe Stock is bundled into Adobe's broader products and services, including Creative Cloud; Adobe's reported company revenue is not a standalone stock-library revenue figure. [Adobe annual reports](https://www.adobe.com/investor-relations/financial-documents.html)
- Alamy's public About Us page identifies it as a global stock and editorial marketplace, but does not publish revenue or market-share data. [Alamy About Us](https://www.alamy.com/about-us)

### Inferences
- Shutterstock is clearly one of the principal global generalist stock platforms, alongside Adobe Stock and Getty's Getty Images/iStock brands, but assigning precise global share percentages based on publicly verifiable like-for-like data would be false precision.
- Any market-sizing figures from commercial research firms should be checked for definitions: some include stock photography only, while others aggregate video, licensing, design software, image services, or AI datasets.

### Gaps
- No independently audited, comparable worldwide stock-content revenue or transaction-share dataset was identified for 2026.
- Public filings do not cleanly isolate each relevant marketplace brand's licensing revenue, customer count, or geographic market share.

## How do Shutterstock and its competitors differentiate?

### Takeaway
The market divides broadly between high-volume, broad-catalog self-service (Shutterstock and Adobe Stock), premium/editorial and rights-rich content (Getty Images), value-plus-premium tiering (iStock), and breadth/diversity/editorial utility (Alamy). Adobe has a distinctive workflow advantage through Creative Cloud integration; Getty's core moat is exclusive editorial, sports/entertainment access, archives, and enterprise rights services.

### Cited Findings
- Shutterstock's licensing platform covers photos, vectors, illustrations, footage, and music, and the company also markets enterprise and data-licensing services. [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/); [Shutterstock annual reports](https://investor.shutterstock.com/financials/annual-reports-and-proxy/default.aspx)
- Adobe Stock is integrated with Adobe's creative applications and offers assets for use across Creative Cloud workflows, giving designers direct access from familiar authoring tools. [Adobe Stock](https://stock.adobe.com/); [Adobe Creative Cloud](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud.html)
- Getty Images positions its offer around premium creative and editorial imagery, exclusive partnerships (including major media, sports, and entertainment entities), archival content, and enterprise services. [Getty Images About Us](https://www.gettyimages.com/about-us)
- Getty describes iStock as a value-oriented offer with both Basic and Premium collections; its current site also states that it does not accept contributor AI-generated visuals. [iStock](https://www.istockphoto.com/); [iStock AI-free imagery policy](https://www.istockphoto.com/legal/ai-free-imagery-policy)
- Alamy emphasizes diverse global contributors, creative and editorial stock, live news, archival material, and a very large searchable collection; it states that it has more than 150,000 content creators. [Alamy About Us](https://www.alamy.com/about-us/)
- Getty's portfolio includes Unsplash, a free/open image discovery brand as well as paid premium and subscription products; this expands its funnel and audience beyond traditional paid stock buyers. [Getty Images About Us](https://www.gettyimages.com/about-us); [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/)

### Inferences
- Adobe's distribution advantage is workflow adjacency, rather than proof that it has the largest paid stock business: Creative Cloud users can discover and license assets in the tools where they create.
- Getty competes less directly on lowest-cost commodity downloads and more on hard-to-replicate editorial access, premium collections, archive depth, brand partnerships, and licensing/rights confidence.
- Shutterstock's strategic breadth (creative stock, footage, music, enterprise, and data licensing) helps it serve multiple use cases, although corporate breadth also makes its financials less comparable to a pure stock marketplace.
- iStock's no-AI-generated-content policy may appeal to buyers seeking human-created provenance, while competing libraries' AI policies and safeguards vary by product and time; evaluate current terms asset-by-asset.

### Gaps
- Public product pages do not offer standardized quality, search relevance, conversion, or rights-claim outcomes across services.
- Adobe's exact proportion of asset licensing originating inside Creative Cloud versus web purchase is not public in a directly comparable form.

## What can be said about buyer and contributor reach?

### Takeaway
All major named competitors have global online storefronts and serve individuals through enterprise buyers, but hard buyer counts are inconsistently disclosed. Contributor/network figures are useful directional evidence only: Alamy publishes 150,000+ content creators; Getty cites a network of 340,000+ creators for custom content; the figures describe different populations and must not be ranked as equivalent marketplace-contributor totals.

### Cited Findings
- Alamy states it works with more than 150,000 content creators globally and offers creative, editorial, vector, 360-degree, and video content. [Alamy About Us](https://www.alamy.com/about-us/)
- Getty's custom-content page advertises access to a global network of more than 340,000 creators. This is a custom-content network measure, not necessarily the count of active stock contributors. [Getty Images Custom Content](https://www.gettyimages.com/enterprise/custom-content)
- Getty describes Getty Images, iStock, and Unsplash as brands serving brands, businesses, and organizations, with offerings ranging from premium/editorial to broader creative imagery. [Getty Images About Us](https://www.gettyimages.com/about-us)
- Shutterstock publishes investor materials and offers consumer, business, and enterprise licensing channels; exact active buyer and contributor counts should be taken from the latest annual report rather than inferred from the size of its searchable library. [Shutterstock investor relations](https://investor.shutterstock.com/); [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/)
- Adobe Stock is sold globally online and is integrated with Adobe's creative software ecosystem, extending its potential buyer access through Creative Cloud users; Adobe does not provide a directly comparable standalone Stock buyer count on the cited product page. [Adobe Stock](https://stock.adobe.com/); [Adobe Creative Cloud](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud.html)
- iStock's site offers self-service plans and pricing, while Getty provides enterprise solutions, API integration, Premium Access, and custom content, indicating a range from individual/small-business purchasing to managed enterprise workflows. [iStock plans and pricing](https://www.istockphoto.com/plans-and-pricing); [Getty enterprise](https://www.gettyimages.com/enterprise)

### Inferences
- Reach advantage differs by buyer type: Adobe benefits from creator-software distribution; Shutterstock from broad self-service and enterprise distribution; Getty from premium enterprise, editorial, and partner channels; Alamy from global contributors and long-tail/editorial breadth; iStock from a lower-priced Getty-affiliated tier; Unsplash from free discovery and broad web exposure.
- Raw contributor counts, asset counts, and registered users are not interchangeable measures of reachable supply or demand. Active contributors, licensable unique assets, active paying customers, and repeat purchasing would be more decision-useful, but are not disclosed consistently.

### Gaps
- Current 2026 active buyer counts, active contributor counts, geographic revenue mix by brand, and comparable paid-download volumes are not consistently public across the group.
- The 150,000+ Alamy creator figure and Getty's 340,000+ custom-content creator network describe different scopes; no conversion to comparable active marketplace supply is warranted.
