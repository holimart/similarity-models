# Open-weight vision-language models for invoice and receipt understanding

## Which models are credible candidates, and what do their document results establish?

### Takeaway
Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, GLM-4.1V and Gemma 3 merit a task-specific evaluation; Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL explicitly emphasize structured document extraction, and Qwen3 adds stronger OCR/document claims. Published DocVQA/OCR scores are useful screens but are not invoice/receipt field-extraction accuracy: they do not establish exactness on totals, tax arithmetic, line-item association, or abstention. Include OCR-oriented compact models such as GOT-OCR2 and PaddleOCR-VL in a hybrid pipeline comparison, although they are not general-purpose JSON extractors.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen2.5-VL's card explicitly names structured output for invoices, forms, and tables; it supports dynamic image resolution and stable JSON outputs for visual localization. Its published 7B results include DocVQA test 95.7, InfoVQA 82.6, OCRBench 864, and TextVQA 84.9. These are model-team-reported benchmark results, not invoice key-value extraction. [Qwen2.5-VL-7B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)
- Qwen2.5-VL model card says the family has 3B, 7B and 72B options; the HF 7B instruct checkpoint is tagged Apache-2.0 and lists 8B parameter count in repository metadata. It is serveable with vLLM and SGLang. [Qwen2.5-VL model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)
- Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is listed Apache-2.0, BF16 and 9B parameters on HF; the card describes expanded OCR across 32 languages and improved long-document structure parsing, 256K native context, and dynamic deployment via vLLM/SGLang. Treat these as vendor capability claims unless validated on the target task. [Qwen3-VL-8B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct)
- The Qwen3-VL HF evaluation area reports ParseBench overall 46.8 for the `qwen3vl_layout` pipeline and lists text-content 89.5 and formatting 66.8 (asterisked). ParseBench is a document-parsing benchmark with separate chart, layout, table, text-content and text-formatting subsets, not an invoice benchmark; check pipeline and benchmark version before comparing. [Qwen3-VL card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct); [ParseBench dataset/leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/datasets/llamaindex/ParseBench)
- InternVL3.5 has 1.1B, 2.3B, 4.7B, 8.5B, 15.1B, 38.4B dense and larger MoE variants; HF format and Apache-2.0 licensing are identified on the official card. Authors report OCR/chart/document evaluations, dynamic high resolution (up to multiple image tiles), vLLM and LMDeploy serving, and 8B model 8.5B total parameters (0.3B vision, 8.2B language). They report up to 30B deployable on one A100, 38B on two A100s, 235B on eight A100s; those are author deployment guidance, not universal minimums. [InternVL3.5 model card](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B)
- InternVL's reported OCR/document score charts are image assets on its official card and aggregate several benchmarks; avoid quoting an unreadable chart value without reviewing the original graphic and protocol. The model card documents dynamic high-resolution tiling and says its Flash variant reduces visual-token count by 50% while preserving nearly 100% of InternVL3.5 performance, according to the authors. [InternVL3.5 model card](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B)
- Pixtral-12B (12B language decoder plus 400M vision encoder) is Apache-2.0, supports variable-resolution images, and the official model card reports DocVQA ANLS 90.7 in a shared evaluation pipeline, versus Qwen2-VL-7B 94.5 in the same table. The model card provides no invoice schema extraction score. [Pixtral-12B model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Pixtral-12B-2409)
- Llama 3.2 Vision 11B and 90B are commercially usable subject to Meta's Llama 3.2 Community License, attribution/use-policy clauses, and access requirements; EU restrictions apply to multimodal rights under the card. Meta reports DocVQA test ANLS 88.4 (11B) and 90.1 (90B) for instruction-tuned models. The card says image+text use is officially supported in English only. [Llama 3.2 11B Vision card](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct)
- Gemma 3 offers multimodal 4B, 12B and 27B models (and 1B text-only); its model card reports DocVQA validation 72.8 / 82.3 / 85.6 for 4B / 12B / 27B respectively. It uses the Gemma Terms of Use rather than a standard permissive OSI license and requires accepting terms to download from HF. Images are normalized to 896×896 and encoded to 256 tokens. [Gemma 3 model card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it); [Gemma Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms)
- GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking (HF reports 10B params) is MIT-licensed, bilingual Chinese/English, supports up to 4K images and 64K context. Its authors report best results among 10B VLMs on 23/28 benchmark tasks and outperformance of Qwen2.5-VL-72B on 18, but headline counts are not substitutes for an invoice-specific matched evaluation. [GLM-4.1V official HF card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01006)
- GOT-OCR2.0 is a 0.6–0.7B Apache-2.0 OCR-focused model, with plain and formatted OCR, region selection, multi-crop and multi-page options; the HF Transformers implementation states its model output is plain text (formatted mode can yield markup), not guaranteed invoice JSON. Useful as an OCR component/low-cost baseline, not an equivalent general VLM. [GOT-OCR2.0 card](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/GOT-OCR-2.0-hf); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01704)
- DocVQA is document visual question answering, with ANLS as a benchmark metric; the model-card numbers use varying splits (test vs validation) and evaluation conventions, so do not rank them naively across cards. OCRBench measures OCR-oriented VLM capability and ParseBench adds layout, table, chart, text-content and formatting parsing dimensions. [DocVQA](https://www.docvqa.org/); [OCRBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07895); [ParseBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/llamaindex/ParseBench)
- ParseBench HF card shows 169k examples across five subsets (charts, layout, tables, text content, text formatting); its live leaderboard and benchmark repo are evolving. Qwen3-VL appears in its recorded evaluation outputs, but model/version/pipeline differences and dynamic leaderboard state warrant rerunning against a fixed commit. [ParseBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/llamaindex/ParseBench)

### Inferences
- Best initial candidates for direct image-to-JSON trials: Qwen2.5-VL-7B (explicit invoice structured-output positioning and strong OCR/doc results), Qwen3-VL-8B (newer OCR/document claims and Apache-2.0), InternVL3.5-8B (high-resolution document capabilities, Apache-2.0), and GLM-4.1V-9B when Chinese or reasoning quality matters. Treat Pixtral, Gemma and Llama Vision as credible comparators, not presumed invoice leaders.
- Use at least two tracks in evaluation: (a) single-pass VLM image→schema-constrained JSON; (b) OCR/layout extraction followed by text/regions→LLM JSON. OCR specialists may give auditable text and lower weight memory, while general VLMs can resolve semantic relationships; quality and operating cost should be measured end to end.
- A practical invoice rubric should include exact field match for vendor/date/currency/subtotal/tax/total, line-item row and quantity-price association, arithmetic consistency, missing/illegible value abstention, JSON parse/schema validity, evidence/crop citation, latency, throughput and language. Evaluate photographed receipts, thermal print, skew, blur, low resolution, handwriting, multi-page invoices and unseen vendor templates separately.

### Gaps
- No credible, standardized published head-to-head invoice/receipt image→JSON benchmark was located for all named models. DocVQA/InfoVQA/OCRBench and generic ParseBench cannot directly answer invoice-field accuracy, receipt long-tail performance, confidence calibration, or hallucination/abstention behavior.
- There are no universal measured VRAM requirements or serving prices in the cited model cards. Exact runtime memory depends on framework, weight format, context/KV cache, image resolution/tiling, batch size, concurrency, and allocator overhead.

## What do quantization, structured output and serving economics look like?

### Takeaway
Weight-only VRAM estimates are easy to approximate, but production VRAM and per-document cost are workload-specific. The most useful distinction is between model capability to emit JSON and serving-layer constrained decoding: schema/grammar enforcement can ensure syntactic validity, but cannot ensure field truth.

### Cited Findings
- Approximate raw weight memory (decimal GB, before vision activations, KV cache, runtime buffers, CUDA context and fragmentation): BF16 ≈ 2 bytes/parameter; 8-bit ≈ 1 byte/parameter; 4-bit ≈ 0.5 byte/parameter. Based on published parameter counts this implies Qwen2.5-VL 7B: ~14/7/3.5 GB; Qwen3-VL 8B: ~18/9/4.5 GB using HF's 9B metadata; InternVL3.5 8B: ~17/8.5/4.3 GB; Pixtral 12.4B total: ~25/12.4/6.2 GB; Llama Vision 11B: ~22/11/5.5 GB; Gemma 3 12B: ~24/12/6 GB; GLM-4.1V 10B: ~20/10/5 GB. These are arithmetic estimates, not measured VRAM requirements. Vision towers and quantization metadata can shift real values; KV and high-resolution visual tokens can add substantial memory.
- InternVL3.5 card explicitly documents bitsandbytes 8-bit loading for 8B and multi-GPU device-map options. Qwen2.5-VL docs expose resolution controls (example 256–1280 visual tokens recommended as speed/quality trade-off). Both show that resolution/token count and quantization are serving knobs. [InternVL3.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B); [Qwen2.5-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)
- Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, Pixtral and Gemma 3 cards provide vLLM serving examples; Qwen and InternVL also cite SGLang or LMDeploy options. Availability by backend/version should be verified against the backend's current model support matrix. [Qwen2.5-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [Qwen3-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct); [InternVL3.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B); [Pixtral](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Pixtral-12B-2409); [Gemma 3](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it)
- vLLM supports structured outputs with JSON schema/grammar constraints in its OpenAI-compatible serving APIs; this is a decoding/runtime feature, separate from the VLM's own model-card claim of stable JSON. [vLLM structured outputs documentation](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/structured_outputs.html)
- Serving cost per document is not stated as a stable figure by the model authors; cloud GPU rental rates and throughput change by provider/region/hardware. Cost can be calculated from measured throughput: hourly GPU instance price ÷ successful documents/hour, adding preprocessing, retries, storage and human review. For API-hosted model access, pricing is provider- and date-specific. [vLLM benchmark documentation](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/benchmarking/)
- Mistral's Pixtral card recommends vLLM ≥0.6.2 and gives adjustable max sequence/image limits; it notes lowering max sequences or max model length for low-VRAM GPUs. [Pixtral model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Pixtral-12B-2409)

### Inferences
- 4-bit weights bring the 7–12B candidates into the approximate 4–7GB weight-only range, often enabling a single 12–16GB GPU for batch-1 inference, but high-resolution invoice images, long KV contexts and engine overhead make this a starting estimate only. Validate the exact backend/quantization combination before hardware selection.
- A useful cost bake-off is fixed-resolution and fixed-output-token, same GPU class/backend, warmed-up models, realistic concurrency, and both cost per successful invoice and field-level exact-match per dollar. Report latency p50/p95 and retries; reasoning variants may use far more output tokens than concise extraction prompts.
- Schema-constrained decoding should be paired with null/unknown values, explicit evidence snippets or normalized bounding boxes, arithmetic post-checks, and field confidence. Valid JSON alone is not evidence of correct extraction.

### Gaps
- No current, comparable public benchmarks were found for invoice throughput, tokens/sec at the same resolution, or per-document GPU cost across these exact model families and serving stacks.
- Quantized VLM support is uneven across architecture/backend versions; the existence of community quantization files is not proof of equivalent output accuracy or supported production serving.

## Which licenses and technical limitations should affect model selection?

### Takeaway
“Open weights” does not mean a common license. Qwen, InternVL, Pixtral and GOT are Apache-2.0; GLM is MIT; Gemma has Google's custom terms; Llama Vision has a custom community license and specific geographic and use conditions. Audit the actual checkpoint and downstream distribution terms rather than relying on a family label.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL checkpoints cited here are Apache-2.0; the official 8B/7B cards also document supported loading/serving and dynamic image resolution. [Qwen2.5-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [Qwen3-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct)
- InternVL3.5 HF card lists Apache-2.0 and offers HF-standard weights alongside its original format; some custom-code checkpoints require `trust_remote_code=True`, so deployment teams should review repository code and pin revisions. [InternVL3.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B)
- Pixtral-12B is Apache-2.0; its card reports no moderation mechanisms and recommends production serving with vLLM. [Pixtral](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Pixtral-12B-2409)
- GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking is MIT licensed and bilingual Chinese/English, per the official card. [GLM-4.1V](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking)
- Gemma 3 distribution is governed by Google Gemma Terms; downloads require accepting usage terms on HF. Terms should be reviewed for commercial, redistribution and derivative-model requirements. [Gemma 3 card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it); [Gemma terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms)
- Meta's Llama 3.2 Community License includes redistribution attribution, acceptable-use obligations, and an additional license trigger for licensees above 700M monthly active users; its model card says multimodal model rights are not granted to individuals/companies domiciled/principal-place-in-EU (with an end-user product exception). [Llama 3.2 card/license](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct)
- Official benchmark records differ across card versions, test/validation splits and metrics. For instance, Pixtral reports DocVQA ANLS and Gemma reports validation score; Llama and Qwen report test ANLS. Avoid direct cross-card rank claims without identical inference prompts, image settings, splits, and scoring. [Pixtral](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Pixtral-12B-2409); [Gemma](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it); [Llama Vision](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct); [Qwen2.5-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)
- High resolution and dynamic tiling are important for small print and dense tables, but increase visual-token count/compute; Gemma 3 instead documents a normalized 896×896 image representation and 256 tokens/image. [Qwen2.5-VL](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [InternVL3.5](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3_5-8B); [Gemma 3](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it)
- DocLayNet consists of 80,863 human-annotated document pages across finance reports, science, law, tenders, manuals and patents; it evaluates layout segmentation, not extraction of invoice/receipt values. [DocLayNet repository](https://github.com/DS4SD/DocLayNet); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01062)

### Inferences
- Receipts are unusually sensitive to low contrast, narrow thermal-paper layouts, tiny totals, clipped edges and line-item row association. Models that do well on page-level DocVQA may still misread a single decimal, omit negative signs, confuse subtotal/tax/total, or invent absent values. Post-validation and targeted test data are essential.
- For localization/evidence, explicitly ask the model to provide source text and image coordinates per field, then verify those coordinates against the image. VLM explanations alone are not reliable proof.
- Before a production decision, pin model commit, tokenizer/processor, framework version, image pixel limits, quantization recipe, system prompt and constrained-decoding schema; rerun a held-out, de-identified evaluation set when any changes.

### Gaps
- Exact current license terms can evolve; this note records the cited cards as accessed during research and is not legal advice. Check upstream LICENSE/terms and model revision before deployment.
- Public results found did not settle whether reasoning-mode checkpoints improve invoice extraction enough to offset output-token/latency cost; compare thinking vs non-thinking modes in the same benchmark.
