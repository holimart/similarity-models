# Commercial invoice and receipt extraction vendors beyond hyperscalers

**Research snapshot: 26 September 2026.** Vendor capability and price statements below are attributed to vendor pages and should be treated as product claims, not comparative test results. Public pricing is often tiered, quote-based, or subject to minimums; compare transaction/page definitions carefully. Independent evidence is limited in the public pages reviewed, so no vendor accuracy claims are treated as independently validated.

## What products are available, what do they cost, and who are they for?

### Takeaway

The market spans API-first extraction (Veryfi, Mindee), configurable workflow/IDP platforms (Nanonets, Klippa/Doxis, ABBYY Vantage), and enterprise AP automation (Rossum, Dext). Of the reviewed products, Veryfi and Mindee publish relatively specific usage prices, Nanonets exposes workflow-block prices, and Rossum publishes an $18,000/year starting tier; Klippa, Dext, and ABBYY generally require a quote for relevant enterprise offerings.

### Cited Findings
- Rossum’s current pricing page lists Starter at **$18,000/year**, unlimited seats, API, extraction, validation, and document archive; Business, Enterprise, and Ultimate are quote-based. Price depends on page/document volume and workflow complexity; minimum contract is one year. The page also advertises a 14-day trial. [Rossum pricing](https://rossum.ai/pricing/)
- Rossum positions itself for scale-ups through global enterprises automating high-volume transactional document workflows, especially AP, with master-data matching, duplicate handling, integrations, and human validation added in higher tiers. These are vendor positioning statements. [Rossum pricing](https://rossum.ai/pricing/)
- Nanonets’ current public pricing describes a free start with $50 credits, then $100/month for 100 credits; published block-run prices are $0.02 (simple), $0.10 (standard AI), and $0.30 (complex AI). It says a typical invoice workflow uses 4–6 blocks, and separately estimates under $2/invoice at $0.30 per complex block; actual flow composition determines cost. Growth and Enterprise pricing is quote/volume based. [Nanonets pricing](https://nanonets.com/pricing)
- Nanonets targets teams automating broader workflows, with no seat/platform fees in its pricing claim. Enterprise tier lists private cloud/on-prem deployment, data residency, SSO, compliance features, support and SLAs; availability is stated on the vendor page and should be confirmed in contracting. [Nanonets pricing](https://nanonets.com/pricing)
- Veryfi offers a free tier up to 100 documents/month. Its current Starter page states a $500/month minimum commitment and API rates of $0.16/invoice and $0.08/receipt, with volume discounts; it notes a 12-month commitment for its pay-as-you-go Starter option. A transaction is one document up to 15 pages, not one page. [Veryfi pricing](https://www.veryfi.com/pricing/)
- Veryfi is positioned as an API/SDK and document-capture infrastructure supplier for developers, fintech, expense, accounting, and AP applications; it also offers a receipt/expense app and no-code/embedded products. API prices should not be confused with the price of its separate expense-management product. [Veryfi pricing](https://www.veryfi.com/pricing/); [Veryfi platform](https://www.veryfi.com/platform/)
- Mindee pricing page lists Starter at $44/month billed annually ($529/year) for 6,000 credits, and Pro at $116/month billed annually ($1,393/year) for the displayed 6,000-credit selection; credits and volume selections are variable. One page equals one credit; a page with confidence score equals 1.5 credits. Enterprise is annual, custom-priced, with a 500,000-credit minimum. The page’s FAQ separately says per-credit pricing starts at $0.05, illustrating that prices depend on selected tier/volume. [Mindee pricing](https://mindee.com/pricing)
- Mindee is API-centric and developer-oriented, with invoice and receipt models, SDKs, confidence scores, polygons, batch/page accounting, and workflow utilities. The Pro tier lists unlimited seats and data-processing localization, while Starter lists one seat; Enterprise adds custom models, SLAs, account management, and premium support. [Mindee pricing](https://mindee.com/pricing)
- Klippa is now marketed under Doxis as **Doxis AI.dp**. Public materials emphasize OCR/IDP APIs, SDKs, custom workflows, and a separate SpendControl expense/AP suite; the Doxis AI.dp pricing page is a contact/request-quote route rather than transparent API rate card. [Klippa OCR/Doxis AI.dp](https://www.klippa.com/en/ocr/); [Doxis AI.dp pricing request](https://www.klippa.com/en/dochorizon/dochorizon-pricing-request/)
- Klippa’s broader suite targets organizations automating document-heavy workflows, including AP, expense management, loyalty programs, and BPO. Its API/SDK offering and end-user SpendControl product are distinct purchasing choices. [Klippa OCR](https://www.klippa.com/en/ocr/); [Klippa products](https://www.klippa.com/en/dochorizon/)
- Dext’s public product positioning centers on bookkeeping/accounting practices and small businesses: receipt and invoice capture, bookkeeping workflows, and accounting integrations. The pricing URL checked did not resolve to a usable current pricing page, so no numeric current price is asserted here. [Dext plans](https://www.dext.com/pricing)
- ABBYY Vantage is an enterprise low-code/no-code IDP platform, with quote-based sales rather than a public per-document rate on the product page. It serves enterprise process automation and can connect to RPA, BPM, ERP, ECM, and other systems. [ABBYY Vantage](https://www.abbyy.com/vantage/)
- ABBYY’s Vantage architecture page describes cloud-first service in ABBYY Cloud regions (Europe, US, Australia) and options for on-premises or private cloud on Azure. The vendor page states pre-trained skills for 150+ use cases; advertised “90% accuracy at the start” is a vendor claim, not a comparative benchmark. [ABBYY Vantage](https://www.abbyy.com/vantage/)

### Inferences
- Veryfi is easiest to budget at low-to-mid API volume if receipt/invoice-specific pricing fits, but its $500/month minimum materially affects small customers above the free allowance. Mindee’s page-credit model is more explicitly page-based and can make multipage invoices or confidence-score usage costlier than a simple per-document comparison suggests.
- Nanonets block pricing can be flexible but requires modeling every workflow step; its own typical-workflow estimate is a vendor estimate rather than a flat per-invoice commitment.
- Rossum, ABBYY, and Doxis/Klippa appear oriented toward broader process transformation and enterprise controls, where public list price alone will not capture implementation, service, integration, or minimum-contract cost.
- Dext is best compared as a bookkeeping workflow product rather than a standalone extraction API. ABBYY Vantage and Rossum are also not direct equivalents to API-only extraction products.

### Gaps
- Public sources reviewed do not provide comparable all-in prices for Rossum, Klippa/Doxis, Dext, and ABBYY; negotiated quotations are needed.
- No common, independently administered pricing/total-cost study was located in the sources reviewed.
- Dext’s pricing page was not fetchable at the checked URL; regional plans and current numeric rates remain unverified.

## What fields, document varieties, and languages are supported?

### Takeaway

Vendors commonly cover invoice and receipt headers, totals, dates, supplier/merchant identity, taxes, and line items, but public materials vary in field-level specificity. Veryfi publishes the strongest specific language statement in the reviewed sources (38 languages); Klippa advertises OCR for 150+ languages, which should not be read as proof that every extracted financial field is supported equally in every language.

### Cited Findings
- Veryfi lists distinct Invoice and Receipt extraction APIs and says its coverage includes 110+ data fields and 91+ currencies across 38 languages. The language list includes, among others, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Hindi, Thai, and Vietnamese. This is a vendor claim; its FAQ does not map each language to each field or document type. [Veryfi pricing / language FAQ](https://www.veryfi.com/pricing/); [Worldwide coverage](https://www.veryfi.com/worldwide-coverage/)
- Veryfi’s receipt product is designed for receipt-level merchant, transaction, tax, payment, and item data, while its invoice product targets invoice-level fields; exact schema/version details should be checked in current API documentation. [Veryfi receipt API](https://www.veryfi.com/receipt-ocr-api/); [Veryfi invoice API](https://www.veryfi.com/invoice-ocr-api/)
- Mindee exposes separate invoice and receipt APIs/products. The current product pages describe normalized structured output rather than a single generic OCR text result; field lists and examples are available in product documentation. [Mindee invoice OCR](https://mindee.com/product/invoice-ocr-api); [Mindee receipt OCR](https://mindee.com/product/receipt-ocr-api)
- Rossum’s pricing page specifically lists automated extraction, validation, custom business logic, master-data matching, and document translation at Enterprise level. It does not publish a fixed invoice/receipt schema or a language-by-field support matrix on that page. [Rossum pricing](https://rossum.ai/pricing/)
- Klippa/Doxis lists invoice, receipt, bank statement, and purchase-order document types among 100+ supported document classes. It advertises 150+ OCR languages and structured JSON/CSV/XML/XLS/UBL outputs, alongside template-free extraction and multipage support. Language support is stated for OCR broadly; field extraction coverage by language is not specified on the cited overview. [Klippa OCR](https://www.klippa.com/en/ocr/)
- ABBYY Vantage supports structured, semi-structured, and unstructured documents, including handwriting, barcodes, and checkboxes; it provides pre-trained skills and allows users to design/train custom skills. Its current overview calls out invoice/financial-document workflows but not a receipt-specific field matrix. [ABBYY Vantage](https://www.abbyy.com/vantage/)
- Nanonets pricing lists data extraction AI and workflow blocks but the pricing page does not enumerate standard invoice/receipt fields or language coverage. [Nanonets pricing](https://nanonets.com/pricing)
- Dext product positioning includes receipts and invoices, but the public page checked did not provide a technical field schema, supported language matrix, or API extraction coverage. [Dext](https://www.dext.com/)

### Inferences
- For a field-level comparison, schema documentation and actual output samples matter more than generalized “AI OCR” or “100+ fields” claims. Receipt fields (merchant, purchase timestamp, tax, tender, line items) and invoice fields (supplier/customer, invoice number/date, payment terms, tax breakdown, PO reference, line items) should be evaluated separately.
- Language counts describe OCR breadth unless vendors explicitly document extraction support per language and document type. Accuracy on multilingual receipts—especially mixed scripts, currencies, and tax conventions—cannot be inferred from headline language counts.
- Rossum, ABBYY, and Nanonets’ extensibility suggests bespoke schemas are possible, but implementation effort and review needs will vary; extensibility is not equivalent to out-of-the-box schema completeness.

### Gaps
- A complete per-field matrix with required/optional fields and line-item structures was not consistently available from high-level vendor pages; validate in API/schema docs and test data.
- The reviewed evidence does not independently verify any vendor’s stated field counts, supported language count, or extraction quality by language.
- No reliable current Dext technical language/field documentation was obtained.

## How do deployment, strengths, weaknesses, and independent evidence compare?

### Takeaway

Deployment choice is a major differentiator: Nanonets advertises private-cloud/on-prem options for Enterprise; ABBYY explicitly documents cloud, private-cloud, and on-prem deployments; most API-first offers are cloud services, with localization/region controls varying by tier. Independent product evidence is sparse in this pass; customer quotes and vendor case studies are useful leads but are not neutral benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- Rossum offers cloud-based document automation with API/email/manual intake, validation, and integrations. Its Enterprise tier lists a preferred cloud location; public pricing does not describe an on-premises deployment option. [Rossum pricing](https://rossum.ai/pricing/)
- Nanonets’ Enterprise pricing explicitly advertises private cloud/on-prem deployment and US/EU/APAC data residency; its lower tiers are presented as hosted SaaS. These are vendor-disclosed tier features. [Nanonets pricing](https://nanonets.com/pricing)
- Veryfi describes APIs and iOS/Android/browser capture SDKs, with cloud API processing and custom/enterprise options; its pricing advertises custom data retention and model training on Growth. Its own statements about deterministic models and superiority to LLMs are vendor opinion/marketing, not independent evidence. [Veryfi pricing](https://www.veryfi.com/pricing/); [Veryfi security](https://www.veryfi.com/security/)
- Mindee is a cloud API with SDKs and regional/data-processing localization as a paid-tier feature; Enterprise includes custom model and SLA options. [Mindee pricing](https://mindee.com/pricing); [Mindee developer documentation](https://docs.mindee.com/)
- Klippa/Doxis offers API, SDK, workflow builder, and human-in-the-loop features. Its OCR overview claims no processed data is stored by default and describes deployment/integration options, but the pricing and security details should be validated contractually for chosen product/region. [Klippa OCR](https://www.klippa.com/en/ocr/); [Doxis API documentation](https://dochorizon.klippa.com/docs/api)
- Dext is delivered as a bookkeeping/expense workflow service with integrations rather than a publicly documented on-prem extraction engine. The source checked does not establish an on-prem deployment or a general-purpose extraction API. [Dext](https://www.dext.com/)
- ABBYY Vantage supports cloud, on-premises, and private-cloud-on-Azure deployment according to ABBYY’s product description, plus REST API and connectors for automation platforms. [ABBYY Vantage](https://www.abbyy.com/vantage/)
- Vendor-described strengths: Rossum emphasizes validation and end-to-end AP workflows; Nanonets emphasizes configurable blocks and workflow automation; Veryfi emphasizes receipt/invoice APIs, SDKs, and broad field/language coverage; Mindee emphasizes developer tooling and page-based transparent pricing; Doxis/Klippa emphasizes broad document coverage and modular workflow/HITL; Dext emphasizes practice bookkeeping workflows; ABBYY emphasizes configurable enterprise skills and deployment flexibility. Sources are each vendor’s own product pages (linked above).
- Independent evidence is not equivalent across providers. G2 and Capterra review pages exist for Mindee and Klippa, and Rossum links to its G2 profile, but review scores/sample sizes can be self-selected and do not measure extraction accuracy on a common dataset. [Mindee G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/mindee/reviews); [Mindee Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/255574/Mindee/reviews/); [Klippa Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/195473/Klippa/reviews/); [Rossum G2](https://www.g2.com/products/rossum/reviews)
- ABBYY references Gartner, Everest Group, IDC, NelsonHall, and Infosource analyst coverage; the product page itself is vendor-hosted and does not expose enough of the underlying assessments to treat the cited leadership language as an independent evaluation in this note. [ABBYY Vantage](https://www.abbyy.com/vantage/)
- A public G2 intelligent document processing category page was not accessible during this research pass (403), and no independently run head-to-head invoice/receipt benchmark was secured. [G2 IDP category](https://www.g2.com/categories/intelligent-document-processing)

### Inferences
- **Strengths by fit:** Veryfi and Mindee are natural candidates for product teams integrating extraction into applications; Rossum and ABBYY fit larger operations seeking governed workflow/validation and integration; Nanonets offers an accessible workflow builder with a visible usage model; Doxis/Klippa offers both modular IDP and adjacent spend/expense products; Dext fits bookkeeping-oriented users who value accounting workflow over raw API control.
- **Likely tradeoffs:** API-first vendors leave downstream workflow, exception handling, and integration design to the buyer; end-to-end platforms may demand more configuration, implementation, and commercial negotiation. This is a category-level inference from the products’ described scope, not a comparative measured result.
- **Evaluation priorities:** Run the same representative scans and PDFs through each shortlisted vendor; score exact field accuracy, line-item completeness, tax arithmetic, confidence calibration, language/script coverage, latency, corrections needed, and total cost under the real page/document and workflow-step definitions. Review residency, retention, and deployment specifics with the vendor before selecting.
- Marketing numbers such as “99% accuracy,” “150+ languages,” and “90% accuracy at the start” should be treated as scoped claims unless accompanied by a reproducible test set, metric definition, and independent validation.

### Gaps
- No neutral, current benchmark comparing these vendors on identical real-world receipts/invoices was found in the sources reviewed. Review websites provide customer sentiment, not controlled extraction testing.
- Deployment details for Veryfi, Mindee, Klippa/Doxis, and Dext may depend on contracted plan, region, data-retention terms, and product SKU; confirm through current security documentation and contract terms.
- No consistent public measures of handwriting, image quality robustness, item-level recall, confidence calibration, exception-review rate, or production SLA were found across vendors.
