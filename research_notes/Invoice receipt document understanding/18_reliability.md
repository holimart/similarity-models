# Reliability failure modes in invoice and receipt understanding

## What fails across OCR, layout models, VLMs, and extraction APIs?

### Takeaway
Invoice/receipt extraction errors occur at multiple layers: text can be missed or misread, spatial relationships can be lost, and semantically plausible fields can be assigned incorrectly or invented. Totals, tax, and line-item association deserve special controls: an output can look structurally valid while being financially wrong. Published benchmarks establish meaningful variation across layouts and extraction tasks, but public evidence does not provide a single cross-vendor error-rate comparison for the full failure-mode list.

### Cited Findings
- The SROIE competition explicitly separates receipt text localization, OCR, and key information extraction as distinct tasks, and introduces 1,000 scanned receipt images—evidence that recognition and semantic extraction are separate failure points rather than one accuracy measure. [Huang et al., ICDAR2019 SROIE competition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10213)
- DocILE provides 6.7k annotated business documents, 100k synthetic documents, and nearly 1M unlabeled documents; its 55 entity classes, line-item recognition task (assigning values to items in tables), and zero-/few-shot layout test cases expose the difficulty of fine-grained extraction and generalization to unseen or rare layouts. [Šimsa et al., DocILE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)
- OCR-based visual document understanding propagates OCR errors into subsequent understanding; Donut was proposed partly to avoid this dependency, but being OCR-free removes a component boundary rather than guaranteeing faithful values. [Kim et al., Donut (ECCV 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15664)
- Layout and table parsing do not guarantee correct invoice semantics. Google Document AI's Form Parser documentation says its table extraction recognizes conventional tables only (without spanning cells), while AWS Textract documents that multi-page expense analysis treats each page as an individual receipt/invoice and does not retain cross-page context. [Google Document AI response handling](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response); [AWS Textract asynchronous processing](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/async.html)
- Repeated or competing fields are a documented taxonomy challenge: Textract describes normalizing labels such as bill number/invoice number/receipt number into one ID, and returns only unique address categories when expense documents lack unique values. This illustrates ambiguity/collision handling, not proof that all repeated fields are resolved correctly. [AWS Textract: Analyzing invoices and receipts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html)
- Small text is a known input constraint. Azure Document Intelligence lists minimum text height of 12 pixels for a 1024×768 image (about 8-point at 150 dpi) and recommends one clear photo or high-quality scan per document. [Azure Document Intelligence invoice model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/invoice?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Rotation and orientation are not simply textual issues: Document AI exposes layout orientation and a transformation matrix for deskewing/normalization, demonstrating these are explicit geometry properties to inspect. The docs describe capabilities, not a guarantee that arbitrary rotation is corrected without error. [Google Document AI response handling](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response)
- Handwriting support is provider- and model-specific. Google lists handwriting support for Enterprise Document OCR and reports token-level handwriting style metadata for supported processor versions; this capability should not be generalized to every invoice extractor or language. [Google Document AI processor list](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/processors-list); [Google Document AI response handling](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response)
- Public material reviewed did not provide a reliable quantified comparison specifically for stamps/overprints, handwritten amounts on receipts, or field-wise hallucination rates among contemporary VLM invoice systems. Treat these as important test strata, not as failure rates established by the sources here. [Gap: no suitable comparative source found]
- API output scores are not interchangeable quality guarantees. AWS returns confidence per detected field, including separate type, label, and value confidence, as well as page and geometry; Google returns text/layout confidence and optional image quality scores/defect labels. Azure output includes confidence and source spans as part of its extraction structure. These are useful evidence signals, but vendor docs do not establish calibrated probabilities of correctness across customers or document distributions. [AWS Textract invoices/receipts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html); [Google Document AI response handling](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response); [Azure invoice model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/invoice?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- VLMs can produce plausible completions, but invoice-specific hallucination frequency is not established by the cited invoice benchmarks. Donut is a generative OCR-free approach and reports strong benchmark results; its paper does not support a blanket claim that generative extraction hallucinates more (or less) than OCR pipelines. Hallucination should be tested through unsupported-value / evidence-span evaluation. [Kim et al., Donut](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15664)

### Inferences
- A mistaken amount may originate in OCR (e.g., digit/decimal confusion), table parsing (wrong row/column), semantic selection (subtotal vs total vs amount due), or generation. Evaluate each stage separately and retain source regions/text so downstream checks can localize the cause. This follows from SROIE's distinct task framing, Donut's stated OCR error propagation, and APIs' separate text, geometry, and field outputs. [SROIE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10213); [Donut](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15664); [AWS Textract](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html)
- Totals and tax are semantically ambiguous when documents contain subtotal, tax by rate, total, amount paid, balance due, discounts, tips, or prior balances. Because supported schemas distinguish these fields, implement explicit field definitions and never map the largest/last amount to “total” by position alone. [AWS Textract expense field taxonomy](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html)
- Line-item errors need row-level and association-level metrics, not just OCR word accuracy or header extraction accuracy: DocILE makes item-to-table assignment an explicit task. [DocILE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)
- Arithmetic checks (line extensions, subtotal, tax, discounts, and payable total) and currency/locale-aware parsing are useful anomaly detectors, but should not silently overwrite source readings: rounding, tax exemptions, deposits, tips, and page continuation can make naive equations invalid. This is an engineering inference; the sources establish separate amount fields and multi-page context limits, not universal accounting equations. [AWS Textract field taxonomy](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html); [AWS async processing](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/async.html)
- For stamps, handwriting, repeated fields, tiny print, rotated/cropped images, and unusual tax layouts, maintain targeted stress-test slices. Aggregate accuracy on conventional printed invoices can conceal failures on these less common subgroups. This is a recommended evaluation design inferred from DocILE's layout diversity/zero-shot cases and vendor input limitations. [DocILE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658); [Azure input requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/invoice?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)

### Gaps
- No primary comparative study was located that reports contemporary OCR, layout-model, VLM, and commercial-API performance side-by-side for totals, tax, line items, stamps, handwriting, rotation, and repeated fields on the same dataset.
- No invoice-specific primary evidence located here quantifies hallucination rates or calibrated correctness probabilities for VLMs and extraction APIs. Avoid translating vendor confidence values into probabilities without local validation.

## Which mitigations and confidence-calibration practices have evidence?

### Takeaway
Evidence supports layered quality controls: improve/assess image readability, preserve OCR text and geometry, evaluate exact field and line-item tasks on diverse document layouts, validate API confidence against labeled examples, and route uncertain/high-impact values to review. General calibration research shows neural-network confidence can be overconfident and that temperature scaling can help classification, but this is not direct proof of calibration for invoice fields or vendor API confidence scores.

### Cited Findings
- Google Document AI can return a document image quality score in [0,1] plus ranked defect labels (examples include cutoff and glare) when enabled. This is an input-quality signal, not an extraction-correctness probability. [Google Document AI response handling](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response)
- Azure recommends a clear photo/high-quality scan and documents a minimum text-height condition; these support capture-quality gates and recapture/escalation for text below supported scale. [Azure Document Intelligence invoice model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/invoice?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google OCR responses expose text at token/line/paragraph granularity, bounding polygons, layout orientation, and confidence; AWS exposes value/label/type confidence and geometry. Retaining these lets applications compare a field to its visual/text evidence rather than accepting a bare JSON value. [Google Document AI response handling](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response); [AWS Textract invoices/receipts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html)
- AWS documents structured expense fields including total, amount due, amount paid, subtotal, tax, service charge, gratuity, discount, and line-item quantity/price/unit-price; explicitly mapping these distinct fields is a mitigation for conflation. [AWS Textract invoices/receipts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html)
- DocILE's task design and test conditions support evaluation with layout-disjoint, zero-shot/few-shot data and explicit line-item metrics, rather than relying on a random split or aggregate entity score alone. [DocILE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)
- The SROIE benchmark separates localization, recognition, and key information extraction. A corresponding production evaluation can track detection/coverage, character or word recognition, field exact match, and table row/cell association separately. [SROIE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10213)
- Guo et al. found modern neural networks can be poorly calibrated and report that temperature scaling, a one-parameter post-processing method, is effective on most tested classification datasets. This supports calibrating locally measured model scores on held-out labeled data; transfer to field extraction, sequence generation, or hosted API scores must be experimentally verified. [Guo et al., ICML 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)
- AWS explicitly supplies confidence scores, and Google supplies OCR confidence and image-quality/defect data. The documentation does not promise score calibration; assess reliability diagrams, Brier score/log loss where probabilities are meaningful, and precision/coverage at review thresholds on a representative validation set. The metrics recommendation is an inference from confidence-calibration research, not a vendor claim. [AWS Textract](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html); [Google Document AI](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response); [Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)
- AWS notes that expense analysis handles each page independently and has no cross-page context. For multi-page invoices, application-level grouping and cross-page aggregation/reconciliation are necessary if the business record spans pages. [AWS Textract asynchronous processing](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/async.html)

### Inferences
- Use confidence as a triage feature rather than an accept/reject truth score. Calibrate separately by field type (total, tax, invoice ID, line items), document family, image-quality band, language, and extraction route; monitor drift after provider/model version changes. This follows from the general calibration findings and the diverse layouts/field granularity established by DocILE. [Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599); [DocILE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05658)
- Review routing should consider financial impact and independent consistency signals, not confidence alone: a high-value total with a conflicting subtotal/tax arithmetic check, missing source span, low image quality, or ambiguous repeated values should be reviewed even if its model score is high. This is a proposed control based on distinct field-confidence outputs and quality signals documented by providers. [AWS Textract](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/invoices-receipts.html); [Google Document AI](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/handle-response)
- Require provenance per field: normalized value, verbatim extracted text, page and bounding region, model/version, confidence, and validation status. For VLMs, require cited evidence locations/snippets and fail closed to “not found/needs review” where the image does not support an answer. These are safeguards derived from APIs' source geometry/spans and the known OCR-error/hallucination evaluation gap, not measured universal improvements.
- Evaluate extraction with field exact match and normalized numeric correctness; line-item precision/recall and row association; omission and unsupported-value rates; and selective risk/coverage for automatic acceptance. Report subgroup results for low resolution, rotation, handwriting, stamps, and layout novelty, plus confidence calibration. These metrics address failure modes not captured by a single document-level accuracy number.

### Gaps
- The cited temperature-scaling study is general neural classification work, not invoice entity extraction, OCR token confidence, or commercial API calibration. Field-specific calibration effectiveness remains to be measured on a deployment-representative labeled set.
- Vendor docs describe capabilities, fields, and confidence outputs but do not provide independent, apples-to-apples reliability results or calibrated thresholds for financial automation.
- Evidence reviewed does not establish one preprocessing method (deskew, rotation correction, sharpening, or super-resolution) as reliably improving all receipt/invoice models. Benchmark such transformations on in-domain scans; avoid treating image enhancement as ground truth recovery.
