# Supervised ML for Invoice and Receipt Understanding Before/Alongside LLMs

## What supervised extraction methods were used, and what field-level results are reported?

### Takeaway
Before generative LLM extraction, production systems combined OCR with rules, statistical sequence taggers, and recurrent neural networks; later document models added 2-D layout and image representations to token-classification heads. The strongest directly comparable evidence found here is CloudScan's invoice field F1 and benchmark-level LayoutLM results, but published benchmark scores are not interchangeable with business-grade exact-value accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- A conventional sequence-labeling formulation takes OCR tokens in reading order and predicts a tag per token (often BIO labels such as B-INVOICE_NUMBER/I-INVOICE_NUMBER/O); a CRF adds learned transition scores so adjacent labels form coherent spans. OCR/layout features such as token shape, neighboring words, line position, and bounding-box relations help disambiguate repeated dates, amounts, and addresses. This approach is useful when there is a meaningful text sequence and labeled token spans, but depends on OCR segmentation/order and the chosen feature/model design. The historical CloudScan paper describes a recurrent sequence model for invoice analysis, compared against a logistic-regression production baseline, and reports the challenge of generalizing across layouts. — [CloudScan (Palm et al., ICDAR 2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)
- CloudScan evaluated eight important invoice fields on 326,471 invoices. Average F1 was 0.891 for its recurrent neural model and 0.887 for logistic regression on seen layouts; on unseen layouts the recurrent model reached 0.840 versus 0.788. The paper's abstract does not provide per-field numbers, so these should be treated as aggregate scores, not a claim that every field achieved those values. — [CloudScan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)
- LayoutLM jointly represents OCR text and 2-D token layout, with visual features added in the paper's formulation; it is pretrained then fine-tuned for document tasks. On FUNSD form understanding it reported an F1 improvement from 70.72 to 79.27, and on the CORD receipt benchmark from 94.02 to 95.24. These are dataset/task scores, not vendor-style field-by-field invoice extraction measurements. — [LayoutLM (Xu et al., KDD 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13318)
- LayoutLMv2 adds multimodal text-image interaction and spatial-aware self-attention. The paper reports benchmark scores increasing from 0.9493 to 0.9601 on CORD and 0.9524 to 0.9781 on SROIE (receipt/scene-text key-information extraction); its results also improve FUNSD from 0.7895 to 0.8420. — [LayoutLMv2 (Xu et al., ACL 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14740)
- Benchmark headline scores require care: papers may use entity-level precision/recall/F1, different data splits, and different annotation conventions, while production teams often care about exact normalized field values, coverage, and straight-through processing. The cited abstracts give benchmark/aggregate results but not a uniform cross-paper field-level evaluation protocol. — [LayoutLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13318); [LayoutLMv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14740); [CloudScan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)
- Image-based document systems can detect page regions (for example, header, table, line item, total block), recognize text, and then classify/extract fields or associate labels and values. A detector is especially useful for region/table structure, but detection alone does not decide semantic roles such as invoice date versus due date. LayoutParser documents deep-learning components for layout detection and OCR as separable reusable stages; the source is about document image analysis generally, not invoice-specific accuracy. — [LayoutParser (Shen et al., ICDAR 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15348)
- Vendor systems often conceal or abstract the underlying architecture; a buyer should assess the API's field coverage, confidence, evidence geometry, schema/customization path, and evaluation results rather than assume a particular CRF, BiLSTM, or transformer implementation. AWS Textract AnalyzeExpense returns summary fields and line-item groups with detected labels/values, confidence, and geometry, illustrating an out-of-the-box expense schema with evidence. — [Amazon Textract AnalyzeExpense](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/analyzing-document-expense.html)

### Inferences
- CRF/BiLSTM taggers remain sensible when OCR tokens are clean, document families are bounded, and the task is span extraction; LayoutLM-like token-classification heads are more suited to heterogeneous layouts because they can exploit spatial and visual context. Those are architectural expectations, not guaranteed outcome rankings across a given company's data.
- A useful field scorecard should separately report exact-match after normalization, token/span F1, precision/recall, field coverage (including missing predictions), and performance by vendor/template, scan quality, language, and field. Aggregate average F1 can obscure a critical weak field such as tax ID or total due.

### Gaps
- Publicly accessible sources reviewed here did not provide comparable per-field precision/recall for all invoice fields across CRF, BiLSTM, LayoutLM, and image-detector pipelines.
- Public benchmark abstracts provide benchmark averages, but exact model-head details and metrics can vary between benchmark versions and papers; validate against the full paper and benchmark implementation before making quantitative comparisons.

## What workflow and labeled data did supervised systems require?

### Takeaway
The typical pipeline is document intake and classification, OCR with token geometry, field extraction/line-item structuring, normalization and business validation, then confidence-based review and feedback. The supervision burden ranges from token-by-token span annotation for sequence taggers to field-value labeling linked to page regions in commercial custom extractors; representativeness and split design matter as much as raw example count.

### Cited Findings
- Document understanding decomposes naturally into getting text from documents and extracting the text corresponding to required fields; layouts and field patterns vary by document type and use case. Google Document AI describes OCR/layout digitization, extraction of entities and key-value pairs, classification/splitting, dataset preparation, training/evaluation, and structured output as connected capabilities. — [Google Document AI overview](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/overview)
- A sequence-labeling dataset typically requires each OCR token to be tagged as outside or part of a target field, including span boundaries. For CRF/BiLSTM systems this creates consistent tokenization/BIO conventions as an annotation dependency; errors in OCR splitting and token-to-box alignment can propagate. CloudScan demonstrates a different route: training examples can be derived from end-user feedback rather than requiring precise upfront annotation, and its global model aimed to handle unseen invoice layouts. — [CloudScan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)
- Layout-aware transformer fine-tuning uses the OCR text and token bounding boxes, and may also use the page image; target labels are attached to tokens/entities. LayoutLM was pretrained on document data before task fine-tuning, while LayoutLMv2's method explicitly models text, layout, and image together. — [LayoutLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13318); [LayoutLMv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14740)
- Azure Document Intelligence custom extraction asks users to label document examples with desired field values, then train/evaluate a model. Its documentation says five examples of the same form/document type suffice to get started, not that five guarantee adequate quality. It recommends complete forms, differing values, more examples for low-quality images, balanced format/type/structure coverage, and segmenting distinct templates where appropriate. Neural custom models are designed for structured, semi-structured, and unstructured documents, while template models depend on a consistent visual template. — [Azure custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Azure's current documentation distinguishes custom template models (consistent visual layout; variance harms accuracy) from custom neural models (different structures can share a model when they carry the same information). The documented training ceiling for custom neural extraction is 50,000 pages and 1 GB; these are platform limits, not suggested minimum dataset sizes. — [Azure custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google Document AI outlines choosing/creating a processor, training from train/test data or uptraining a pretrained version, sending documents to a prediction endpoint, and receiving structured Document objects. Its platform supports dataset preparation, auto-labeling, schema management, prediction review, and evaluation. — [Google Document AI overview](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/overview)
- AWS AnalyzeExpense supports synchronous analysis and asynchronous start/get operations; output includes summary fields, line-item groups, label/value detections, confidence, and geometry. This shows how extraction evidence can enter a review UI or downstream mapping workflow. — [Amazon Textract AnalyzeExpense](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/analyzing-document-expense.html)

### Inferences
- A robust supervised workflow needs a canonical schema and annotation handbook before labeling: field definition, aliases, null/not-present policy, multiple occurrences, line-item row boundaries, currency/date normalization, and treatment of tax-inclusive totals. Train/validation/test splits should be grouped by vendor or layout when unseen-vendor generalization is the target; random page splits can leak near-duplicate forms and overstate real-world transfer.
- Preserve original pages, OCR text, token boxes, labels, corrected output, and reviewer decisions as linked records. This enables error diagnosis: OCR failure, region detection failure, wrong semantic field, normalization failure, or schema mismatch.
- Use annotation effort strategically: prioritize the target field ontology and diverse vendor/layout samples, then use active review of low-confidence/disagreement cases. A headline count alone does not establish coverage of rare layouts or difficult fields.

### Gaps
- Neither the reviewed academic abstracts nor the vendor documentation gives a universal annotation-hours-per-document or minimum dataset size for reliable extraction across arbitrary vendor diversity.
- Vendor documentation describes supported capabilities and suggested training practices, not independent head-to-head evaluations on a common invoice/receipt corpus.

## What are operational advantages, limitations, and vendor-product tradeoffs?

### Takeaway
Supervised extraction is attractive when schemas are stable, accuracy can be measured and tuned, and the organization can build a feedback loop; it offers predictable structured outputs and often useful evidence locations. Its costs are dataset curation, drift management, model/version operations, and fragility to out-of-distribution layouts or OCR changes. Managed products reduce infrastructure burden but trade some model transparency and portability for integrated training, APIs, and service constraints.

### Cited Findings
- CRFs provide a structured prediction layer that can model label-transition consistency; BiLSTM-CRF variants combine contextual sequence representations with constrained span labeling. Their inputs are commonly OCR text plus engineered/document-context features, so failures in OCR and reading order can limit downstream extraction. CloudScan's comparison shows recurrent models can improve unseen-layout average F1 over a simpler logistic-regression baseline, but did not eliminate the generalization gap (0.840 vs 0.891 seen-layout F1). — [CloudScan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)
- LayoutLM-family models can capture context from neighboring words, 2-D spatial relationships, and visual appearance, which can disambiguate fields that text-only sequence context may miss. They need OCR tokens and normalized bounding boxes in common pipelines; image-aware variants also increase compute and deployment complexity compared with a lightweight linear tagger. The papers establish multimodal architecture and benchmark gains, not a universal production cost comparison. — [LayoutLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13318); [LayoutLMv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14740)
- Image detectors and OCR/layout pipelines are modular: region detection can isolate headers, tables, and line items, and subsequent extraction can consume the detected text/geometry. Modularity enables targeted component replacement, but creates error propagation and cross-stage integration/evaluation work. The available LayoutParser source addresses toolkit reuse, not invoice-specific operational benchmarks. — [LayoutParser](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15348)
- Azure advertises custom template and custom neural models: template models train in documented 1–5 minute ranges and require a model for each variation; neural models support more varied structures and are documented with 30-minute to 12-hour training times. It recommends neural models where supported and appropriate. These are Azure product characteristics and should be rechecked for API/version changes. — [Azure custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google Document AI offers pretrained processors for specialized documents, custom extractors, train/test evaluation and uptraining, plus endpoint-based structured responses; choosing a processor depends on whether the need is OCR, forms/KVP, custom entities, classification, or splitting. — [Google Document AI overview](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/overview)
- AWS Textract AnalyzeExpense is a specialized expense API that extracts summary and line-item data without a customer training workflow in the cited usage instructions; it exposes confidence and geometry, and offers asynchronous processing for documents. — [Amazon Textract AnalyzeExpense](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/analyzing-document-expense.html)
- Historical vendor-trained ML products also used customer operational feedback as supervision. CloudScan's authors describe automatic training data extraction from end-user feedback as a way to avoid precise upfront annotation and report evaluation at large scale (326,471 invoices). — [CloudScan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07403)

### Inferences
- The practical choice is often hybrid rather than one-model-only: general OCR and extraction for broad coverage; deterministic normalization, arithmetic/tax checks, vendor-specific rules or models for recurring formats; and human review for low-confidence/high-impact fields. Confidence is best treated as a routing signal after calibration rather than a guarantee of correctness.
- Vendor-trained products can shorten time to initial deployment and provide stable schemas, support, scaling, and evidence coordinates. They may impose cloud/data residency, pricing, rate/size limits, API schema evolution, and reduced insight into training data/model behavior; verify these contract and service details directly because the sources reviewed do not compare them consistently.
- Self-hosted CRF/BiLSTM/LayoutLM pipelines offer control over model choice, data locality, and evaluation, but require OCR integration, annotation/ML expertise, compute, monitoring, and retraining ownership. A classic tagger can be cheaper and easier to debug for a narrow field set; a pretrained layout transformer can reduce feature engineering but is heavier and still requires task-specific labeled examples.
- Always benchmark on a blind, vendor-disjoint test set and report coverage alongside field-level exact match. The strongest published mean F1 can still be operationally unacceptable if one critical field or long-tail vendor underperforms.

### Gaps
- No reliable independent, contemporary, apples-to-apples product benchmark was found in these sources comparing Google Document AI, Azure Document Intelligence, AWS Textract, and self-hosted CRF/BiLSTM/LayoutLM on the same invoices, field schema, language mix, and document-quality distribution.
- Pricing, regional availability, retention, rate limits, latency, and contractual data handling change over time and are not characterized here; assess current service terms and measure with representative documents before selecting a platform.
