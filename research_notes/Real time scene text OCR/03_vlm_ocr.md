# Open-weight VLMs for scene-text and document OCR

## OCR accuracy evidence and the distinction between reading and OCR

### Takeaway
General VLMs can answer questions about visible text and often score well on OCR-oriented VQA benchmarks, but that is not the same task as complete, repeatable text spotting. For reliable scene-text transcription with per-word/line coordinates, use a detector-recognizer or a model whose documented OCR mode explicitly returns regions; treat ordinary image QA/localization as complementary.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B model-card results report OCRBench 864, TextVQA 84.9, DocVQA 95.7 and InfoVQA 82.6; in the same comparison it leads the listed open models on OCRBench, DocVQA and InfoVQA. These are benchmark scores, not word-level detection recall or end-to-end scene-text spotting metrics. [Qwen2.5-VL-7B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)
- MiniCPM-o 2.6 (8B) reports OCRBench 897 and DocVQA 93.5 in its own model-card table, and advertises OCRBench leadership below 25B parameters. Its card footnotes that some scores use chain-of-thought prompting; scores across cards should not be treated as controlled head-to-head results. [MiniCPM-o 2.6 model card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-o-2_6)
- InternVL1.5 improved OCR/document and Chinese tasks through bilingual scene/document QA data and dynamic tiling at 448×448, from 1 to 40 tiles, permitting up to 4K input. InternVL3 continues dynamic 448-pixel tiling and publishes OCR/document benchmark comparisons; these are VLM comprehension evaluations, not conventional detector recall/precision. [InternVL1.5 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16821); [InternVL3-8B model card](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3-8B)
- Florence-2 is a 0.23B/0.77B promptable vision model. Its card demonstrates `<OCR>` for text and `<OCR_WITH_REGION>` returning quadrilateral coordinates plus recognized labels, an unusually direct combination of transcription and localization among general-purpose models. The card’s headline benchmark tables primarily cover captioning, VQA and object/phrase grounding, and do not provide comparable scene-text word error rates. [Florence-2-large model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Florence-2-large)
- GOT-OCR2.0 is a 580M-parameter end-to-end OCR model aimed at scene and document images. It supports plain/formatted OCR and region-level recognition selected by coordinates or colors; repository examples show coordinate/color guided OCR and multi-crop modes. Its paper claims broad benchmark strength, but public report material does not establish a controlled comparison against modern scene-text detectors on the user’s camera-image distribution. [GOT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01704); [official implementation](https://github.com/Ucas-HaoranWei/GOT-OCR2.0)
- PaddleOCR separates specialist scene OCR (PP-OCR detector + recognizer / text spotting pipeline) from PaddleOCR-VL document parsing. Its current repository advertises PP-OCRv6 tiny/small/medium at 1.5M/7.7M/34.5M parameters, 50 unified languages and 0.13 s on A100 for the medium tier, with +4.6% detection and +5.1% recognition over PP-OCRv5 server. Those are vendor-reported suite results; they cannot be directly ranked against OCRBench/TextVQA scores. [PaddleOCR official repository](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- PaddleOCR-VL-0.9B is a dynamic-resolution document VLM (NaViT-style vision encoder + ERNIE-4.5-0.3B), with 109 languages in the original release and structured document output. The same repository advertises later 1.5/1.6 releases with document parsing scores up to 96.3% on OmniDocBench v1.6 and spotting/coordinate functionality; OmniDocBench is document parsing, not scene-text detection. [PaddleOCR official repository](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR); [PaddleOCR-VL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14528)
- olmOCR/olmOCR-2 is a 7B VLM workflow for converting rendered PDF pages to readable Markdown/text, with table/formatting and OCR benchmark coverage. The repository’s olmOCR-Bench is document-centric (7,000+ cases across 1,400 documents); it does not target real-time camera scene text or produce a standard set of word boxes. [olmOCR repository and benchmark](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr); [olmOCR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18443)
- GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking targets multimodal reasoning and supports bilingual Chinese/English, arbitrary aspect ratios and up to 4K images. Its cited card claims performance over 72B Qwen2.5-VL on 18 varied benchmarks, but does not establish dependable word-level OCR boxes or a scene-text spotting evaluation. [GLM-4.1V model card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking)
- Qwen2/2.5/3-VL support dynamic-resolution image inputs; Qwen2.5-VL advertises bounding boxes/points for visual localization and structured outputs. Such coordinates are generally prompted object/region grounding, not an exhaustive OCR output of every word with confidence and ordering. Qwen3-VL is described in current Transformers docs as a family of dense and MoE VLMs with multiscale visual features; exact OCR performance depends on the checkpoint and should be measured on the target domain. [Qwen2-VL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12191); [Qwen2.5-VL model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [Qwen3-VL Transformers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/qwen3_vl)
- Benchmarks answer different questions: OCRBench/TextVQA reward reading/answering sampled queries; DocVQA/InfoVQA concern document question answering; OmniDocBench evaluates page parsing; scene-text detection/recognition benchmarks measure detection and transcription, commonly via recall/precision/F-score and word/character error. Results should not be collapsed into one “OCR accuracy” rank. [OCRBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07895); [PaddleOCR repository](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR); [olmOCR benchmark](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)
- Typical VLM failure risks follow the generative QA formulation: omission of small/dense text, invented or normalized characters, merged reading order, uncertain text returned with fluent confidence, and coordinate outputs that are prompted rather than calibrated detection results. High resolution/tiling helps preserve small text but increases image tokens, latency and memory. Test these risks directly on target imagery; the cited benchmark results do not quantify all of them. [Qwen2.5-VL model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [InternVL1.5 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16821); [olmOCR repository](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)

### Inferences
- For “what does this sign say?” or reading one selected label in a scene, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, InternVL, GLM and MiniCPM-V/o are suitable candidates: they combine visual context and flexible question answering. A correct answer to a question does not show that all text was found.
- If outputs need text plus coordinates, Florence-2 `<OCR_WITH_REGION>` and PP-OCR’s spotting pipeline offer documented region-level output paths. GOT supports user-directed coordinate/color OCR, but is not equivalent to exhaustive detector-generated boxes. Evaluate coverage, false detections, coordinate error and transcription jointly.
- For continuous camera use, a specialist detector-recognizer is the stronger baseline: it is small, fast, emits boxes/confidence and supports tracking across frames. Invoke a VLM selectively on detected crops for ambiguous reading, semantic interpretation, or user questions. This hybrid reduces autoregressive token generation and makes detection recall independently measurable.
- Fair in-house comparison should fix image resolution/crops, language, decoding, and hardware; report end-to-end latency (including image tiling), text detection recall/precision, normalized edit distance, exact-line accuracy, box IoU/pointing error, and omissions/hallucinations stratified by text height and blur/angle/occlusion.

### Gaps
- No single cited public result cleanly compares all named VLMs and modern detector-recognizer systems on the same camera-scene benchmark, resolution, languages and hardware. Claims of “outperforming mainstream VLMs” from vendor sources are not independent cross-evaluations.
- Benchmark numbers and versions move over time; Qwen3-VL and GLM performance claims should be checked against specific released checkpoints and benchmark protocols before selection.
- There is no dependable public apples-to-apples latency table for these VLMs on a common GPU and image/crop workload. Do not infer real-time throughput from parameter counts alone.

## Model deployment: output, size, GPU fit, speed, languages, license and failure modes

### Takeaway
For one-GPU experimentation, 7–9B VLMs are plausible at 16-bit on a 24GB card only with careful image-token and generation settings; 4-bit can reduce weight memory but does not eliminate visual-token/KV-cache cost. The most compact options are Florence-2/GOT and dedicated OCR networks, while the best-supported multilingual and production scene-text path is PaddleOCR. Published FPS is sparse and setup-specific.

### Cited Findings
- **Qwen2.5-VL:** official 3B/7B/72B family; the 7B card is approximately 8B total parameters in BF16 and Apache-2.0. Inputs support 4–16,384 visual tokens by default; the processor can cap `max_pixels` for speed/memory tradeoff. 7B BF16 weights alone are roughly 16GB decimal before activations, cache and runtime overhead; quantized community variants exist. Card reports OCRBench 864 but no comparable per-image latency. Dynamic resolution can preserve small text while increasing compute. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct)
- **Qwen3-VL:** multiple dense/MoE variants are documented, including 4B Instruct in Transformers configuration; dynamic visual processing and DeepStack features are supported. Model licenses and usable quantizations are checkpoint-specific; consult each exact Hub card. No consistent published scene-text throughput or OCR box guarantee was located. [Transformers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/qwen3_vl); [Qwen3-VL collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/Qwen/qwen3-vl)
- **InternVL:** family spans 1B through 78B (InternVL3); model card provides BF16 and 8-bit loading instructions and a dynamic tiling recipe with up to 12 tiles in its example. InternVL3-8B is Apache-2.0 per card, while its description also says project MIT and Qwen2.5 component Apache-2.0—check component/model licensing in the chosen release. Eight-bit may reduce weight memory, but the card’s example warns large-family deployments need multi-GPU; its size-specific examples and visual tile count matter. No shared hardware latency results. [InternVL3-8B card](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3-8B)
- **GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking:** around 10B total parameters, BF16, MIT, bilingual Chinese/English; supports 64k context and arbitrary aspect ratio/up to 4K. BF16 weights alone are about 20GB decimal; extra memory is required for visual tokens, KV cache and runtime. Quantized derivatives are listed on Hub, but official comparable quantized accuracy/speed data were not located. Reasoning-style generation can produce long outputs and is poorly matched to low-latency transcription unless constrained. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking)
- **Florence-2:** base 0.23B and large 0.77B; MIT; official examples use FP16. OCR and OCR-with-region are explicit prompts, with quadrilateral boxes for the latter. This is substantially smaller than 7–9B chat VLMs and can fit comfortably on a modest GPU, but exact end-to-end camera FPS and supported language inventory are not specified in the cited card. Requires `trust_remote_code=True` in the documented Transformers path. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Florence-2-large)
- **GOT-OCR2.0:** 580M parameters; supports plain/formatted OCR, box/color-guided OCR and multi-crop; official setup reports CUDA 11.8/PyTorch 2.0.1 and uses FlashAttention. Code is Apache-2.0 and training data are marked CC BY-NC 4.0 in the repo; inspect checkpoint/model card license separately before commercial use. Small parameter count suggests low weight memory, but the repository does not provide a common FPS or guaranteed scene-language list. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01704); [repository](https://github.com/Ucas-HaoranWei/GOT-OCR2.0)
- **PaddleOCR / PaddleOCR-VL:** specialist PP-OCRv6 scene models are 1.5M/7.7M/34.5M parameters, support 50 languages in the unified model and the project is Apache-2.0. Vendor claims 0.13 seconds on A100 GPU for medium and 5.2× CPU speedup over PP-OCRv5. By contrast PaddleOCR-VL is a 0.9B document parser with 109 languages in the original release; document model boxes/coordinates and scene OCR spotting interfaces are distinct. Vendor also reports later 1.5/1.6 document checkpoints and capabilities; verify exact model version and task fit. [Official repository](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- **MiniCPM-V / MiniCPM-o 2.6:** o 2.6 uses approximately 8B parameters (SigLIP-400M, Whisper-medium-300M, ChatTTS-200M and Qwen2.5-7B components), Apache-2.0 card; supports >30 languages and up to 1.8M image pixels. Card reports 640 visual tokens at max image size, int4 and GGUF variants, plus OCRBench 897. Actual vision-only memory is less than the omni stack if audio/TTS modules are not initialized. It produces QA/transcription rather than standard exhaustive OCR boxes. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-o-2_6)
- **olmOCR-2:** 7B VLM, Apache-2.0 project, released FP8 checkpoint. Local GPU instructions specify at least 12GB GPU RAM and tested RTX 4090/L40S/A100/H100; it is designed for PDF/page linearization, not streaming camera spotting. The repository reports costs/benchmark scores for a batch document pipeline, not frame latency. [Repository](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr); [checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/allenai/olmOCR-2-7B-1025-FP8)
- OCR model-card scores are not standardized with respect to image resizing, prompt, CoT, benchmark version, or decoding. “Quantized” alone is not a performance guarantee: quantization can change small-character recognition, and VRAM must also cover vision activations, image-token sequence, KV cache, framework buffers and concurrency. [Qwen2.5-VL card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct); [MiniCPM-o card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-o-2_6); [InternVL3 card](https://huggingface.co/OpenGVLab/InternVL3-8B)

### Inferences
- Rough single-GPU fit from parameter count, excluding runtime overhead: Florence-2 and GOT are easy fits; PaddleOCR scene models are edge-friendly; 7B BF16 VLM needs roughly 16GB just for weights, 8–9B roughly 16–20GB, and 4-bit often makes weight loading feasible at about 5GB for 8B, but adequate headroom still depends on image tiling and context. For reliable deployment reserve substantial VRAM beyond weights and benchmark actual peaks.
- Candidates by need: **interactive scene QA:** Qwen2.5/3-VL or InternVL; **reasoning and bilingual scene understanding:** GLM-4.1V; **small promptable text+quads:** Florence-2; **OCR-first unified recognizer:** GOT; **high-throughput scene text with boxes/language coverage:** PaddleOCR PP-OCR; **document/page parsing:** PaddleOCR-VL or olmOCR; **edge-friendly VLM with high image token density and broad language support:** MiniCPM-V/o.
- “Mixed-size text” favors native/dynamic high-resolution encoders or explicit tiling/cropping. For small text far from the camera, cap-resizing the whole image can erase the signal; use a detector to crop regions, or increase image-token budget while accepting a speed/memory penalty.
- License shorthand is insufficient for a product decision. Validate the exact checkpoint license and upstream component/data terms: InternVL components, GOT’s separate data license, and community quantizations can carry different terms from repo code.

### Gaps
- No uniform published FPS/latency comparison across the named models on one GPU, with fixed resolution, batch size, prompt, quantization and accuracy target. Real-time claims require a local benchmark.
- Public cards do not consistently specify per-language scene-text performance, especially for small fonts, scripts outside Latin/Chinese, motion blur, perspective, glare, curved surfaces and occlusion. “Supports N languages” should not be interpreted as equal accuracy across scripts.
- Exact GPU memory depends on serving stack, max visual tokens/crops, output length, beam/search settings, batch/concurrency and quantization implementation. The estimates above are weight-memory arithmetic, not verified peak-VRAM specifications.
