# Open-weight scene-text detection and recognition models

## Which models best handle variable scale, rotation, perspective, and irregular text, and what are their tradeoffs?

### Takeaway
There is no single winner for every condition. For practical, downloadable detection-plus-recognition, PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5 (and newer PP-OCRv6) is the strongest current turnkey family found: small deployable models and multilingual coverage, but detector/recognizer are separate stages and reported benchmarks are vendor results. For curved/irregular text specifically, DB/DB++ segmentation detectors and TextSnake-style representations fit text shapes better than axis-aligned boxes; ABCNet/ABCNet++ and newer end-to-end spotters jointly model curved geometry and text. PARSeq is a compelling lightweight recognizer for rotated crops. Model weights being downloadable does not itself establish that their license permits commercial use; check the exact model card/weight terms.

### Cited Findings
- **DB / differentiable binarization (DBNet).** DB integrates an adaptive threshold/binarization operation into a segmentation network, improving conversion of pixel maps into text regions and supporting curved text. The paper reports ResNet-18 at 82.8 F-measure and 62 FPS on MSRA-TD500; this is a paper-specific benchmark and not a modern universal deployment speed. [DB paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08947)
- Official DB implementation supplies pretrained TD500 and Total-Text ResNet-18/50 checkpoints via Google Drive/Baidu, and gives polygon-aware results for Total-Text. Its repo explicitly notes GPU forward plus CPU post-processing, with speed dependent on both CPU and GPU; training recipe uses older PyTorch/CUDA dependencies. [DB repo and weight links](https://github.com/MhLiao/DB)
- **DBNet++** augments DB with adaptive scale fusion (ASF), aimed at scale variation; official author repository links trained models separately. Its reproducible code/checkpoint supply is less convenient than a maintained packaged model zoo. [DBNet++ paper/repository](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10304), [author repository](https://github.com/MhLiao/DB)
- **TextSnake** is a geometry representation for arbitrarily shaped text, including curved instances, based on centerlines, local radii and orientations rather than a single rectangular box. This is a detector, not a recognizer; polygon/centerline post-processing and downstream crop/recognition are still required. [TextSnake paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01544), [official implementation](https://github.com/princewang1994/TextSnake) (availability and maintenance should be checked before adopting).
- **ABCNet** fits arbitrarily shaped text with Bezier curves and uses BezierAlign to sample text features. The original paper claims real-time spotting and over 10× speedup over earlier methods on Total-Text, while preserving competitive recognition accuracy; it is a joint spotter rather than a plug-and-play detector. [ABCNet paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10200), [AdelaiDet implementation](https://github.com/aim-uofa/AdelaiDet)
- **ABCNet v2** integrates a stronger recognition framework and pseudo-labeling/self-training; it is a direct successor for end-to-end curved-text spotting. It remains a research-stack choice with more complex dependencies than the PaddleOCR pipeline. [ABCNet++ / ABCNet v2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03620), [official repository](https://github.com/aim-uofa/AdelaiDet)
- **PaddleOCR PP-OCRv4/v5 and successor v6.** The maintained PaddleOCR repository distributes deployable detection, direction-classification and recognition models; current repository material announces v6 model tiers (1.5M/7.7M/34.5M parameters) and Hugging Face/ModelScope weight collections. The README reports v6 improvements over v5 and 50-language support for its unified models; these are project-reported results, not independent comparative evaluation. [PaddleOCR repo](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR), [v6 weights](https://huggingface.co/collections/PaddlePaddle/pp-ocrv6), [PaddleOCR 3.0 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05595)
- PP-OCRv5 reports a 2M-parameter multilingual recognizer in the repository and 109-language coverage for multilingual recognition releases; language-specific variants (such as English) are reported to improve on the general model for their target language. Verify that the chosen model is a scene-text recognizer for the desired script, rather than assuming all language packs are interchangeable. [PaddleOCR release notes/model entry points](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- **SVTR** uses visual-transformer feature extraction for scene text recognition and is available in PaddleOCR model configurations. It avoids an explicit recurrent decoder and is a strong recognition backbone, but it does not detect text: it expects a text-line crop and its accuracy depends on rectification, crop quality, vocabulary and training data. [SVTR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00159), [PaddleOCR models](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- **PARSeq** uses permutation language modeling to share weights across autoregressive/non-autoregressive inference and iterative refinement. The authors report 91.9% benchmark accuracy with synthetic training and 96.0% with real-data training, and describe robustness to arbitrarily oriented text; code and pretrained weights are available. It recognizes cropped lines and does not detect/rectify perspective by itself. [PARSeq paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06966), [official code and checkpoints](https://github.com/baudm/parseq)
- **ABINet** combines visual recognition with a language-model component and iterative language/vision alignment; it can correct visually ambiguous text using context, but language priors can also override visually correct unusual strings. It is a recognizer, not a detector, and the official repo provides pretrained models. [ABINet paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06495), [official code](https://github.com/FangShancheng/ABINet)
- **SATRN** is an attention-based recognizer designed to handle perspective distortion and irregular text lines using 2-D attention over image features. It is a crop recognizer rather than a detector and its transformer attention is heavier than compact convolutional recognizers in some deployment regimes. [SATRN paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04396), [official code](https://github.com/clovaai/SATRN)
- **TrOCR** is a pretrained image encoder/Transformer text decoder, with public model weights in Microsoft/Hugging Face model repositories. It is flexible and benefits from large-scale pretraining, but general TrOCR checkpoints are not purpose-built scene-text detector systems; inference/token generation and larger checkpoints can cost substantially more than compact line recognizers. [TrOCR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10282), [Microsoft repository](https://github.com/microsoft/unilm/tree/master/trocr), [Hugging Face model listing](https://huggingface.co/models?search=microsoft%2Ftrocr)
- The PaddleOCR project is Apache-2.0 licensed at the repository level. That is useful for adoption, but separately inspect each downloaded checkpoint/model card and third-party backbone or training-data restrictions; repository license alone should not be assumed to settle every artifact's terms. [PaddleOCR license](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/main/LICENSE)
- OCR leaderboard comparisons are easy to misread: STR results depend materially on training/evaluation datasets, lexicon use, image preprocessing and module choices. Baek et al. identify inconsistent protocols as a source of misleading model comparisons. Compare models on the target corpus with the same crop/resize, lexicon, vocabulary, and metric. [STR comparison analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906)

### Inferences
- For irregular/curved text, a polygon/segmentation/Bezier detector (DB/DB++, TextSnake, ABCNet-family) should generally preserve geometry better than a detector constrained to horizontal rectangles. Perspective distortion still needs rectification or a recognizer tolerant to 2-D distortion; detection geometry alone cannot ensure correct reading.
- For robust end-to-end deployment under varied content, begin with PP-OCRv5/v6’s official pipeline and choose its appropriate language/model tier, then compare on real target images. If curvature is the dominant failure mode, evaluate a polygon detector or ABCNet-style spotter; if rotated crops are detected but misread, test PARSeq/SATRN/ABINet on those same crops.
- A two-stage detector-plus-recognizer is operationally flexible and lets each model be swapped independently. An end-to-end spotter can improve geometry-to-text integration but usually raises implementation, training and checkpoint compatibility costs.
- “Open weights” is not synonymous with permissive commercial licensing. DB, ABCNet, PARSeq and other research repositories expose code/weights, but check repository license files, paper/data terms and individual checkpoint cards before commercial deployment.

### Gaps
- No uniform current benchmark was found that tests all listed systems on the same varied-scale, rotation, perspective and curved-text dataset with identical hardware and post-processing. Published numbers above are not directly rankable.
- Precise checkpoint file sizes and guaranteed download URLs for every legacy research model (especially TextSnake, ABINet, SATRN and TrOCR variants) were not consistently verifiable from the retrieved primary pages; links point to official model/code entry points rather than asserting a specific artifact size.
- No primary source provided a general, reproducible inference VRAM requirement for these architectures. Inference memory is shaped by input image dimensions, batch size, precision, backend and framework workspace, so the paper/model parameter count is not a VRAM figure.

## Which downloadable weights, licenses, model sizes, and VRAM footprints are evidenced for deployment?

### Takeaway
The best documented deployment path in the reviewed sources is PaddleOCR’s official model zoo and downloadable PP-OCR weights. Most research models make code and checkpoints available, but the evidence retrieved does not support a trustworthy universal VRAM number or a complete commercial-license matrix. Keep weight-file disk size, parameter count, peak inference memory and runtime package size as separate measurements.

### Cited Findings
- PaddleOCR provides an open repository under Apache-2.0 and current downloadable model collections. Its latest README describes three v6 parameter tiers—tiny 1.5M, small 7.7M and medium 34.5M—and identifies weights on Hugging Face/ModelScope. [Repository/license/model links](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- Parameter count yields only a lower-bound scale for raw weights: at FP32, 1.5M, 7.7M and 34.5M parameters correspond to about 6, 31 and 138 MB of parameter data respectively (decimal units), before serialization metadata; FP16 halves that raw parameter storage. This arithmetic does **not** equal checkpoint file size or peak VRAM. [PaddleOCR v6 tiers](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- PP-OCR is specifically presented as an ultra-light system; its original paper reports an overall model size of 3.5 MB for 6,622 Chinese characters and 2.8 MB for 63 alphanumeric symbols, with separately released detector, direction classifier and recognizer. These are original PP-OCR-era artifacts, not guarantees about later v4/v5/v6 packages. [PP-OCR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09941)
- DB repository lists downloadable pretrained DB/DB++ checkpoints and benchmark configurations. Reported model choice/input scale varies by dataset; for example ICDAR 2015 runs use short-side 736 or 1152. The project states speed includes GPU model execution and CPU post-processing, so deployment cost is not captured by neural-network FLOPs alone. [DB repository](https://github.com/MhLiao/DB)
- PARSeq paper states code, pretrained weights and training data are available, through its official repository. Checkpoint and runtime requirements depend on selected model/configuration and inference/refinement mode. [PARSeq official repo](https://github.com/baudm/parseq)
- TrOCR model variants are downloadable through Microsoft/Hugging Face; model sizes vary significantly by encoder/decoder scale. Do not attribute one memory number to the family. [Microsoft TrOCR](https://github.com/microsoft/unilm/tree/master/trocr), [Hugging Face model listing](https://huggingface.co/models?search=microsoft%2Ftrocr)
- DBNet paper reports one historical speed point (ResNet-18, MSRA-TD500, 62 FPS), but not an inference VRAM requirement in its abstract; results from other implementations/hardware will differ. [DB paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08947)
- The official DB implementation’s model weights are linked through external Google Drive/Baidu storage rather than a package registry; its stated setup depends on PyTorch 1.2 and CUDA 9+ in the README. This indicates reproducibility/maintenance overhead relative to a maintained deployment toolkit. [DB repository](https://github.com/MhLiao/DB)
- PaddleOCR supports conversion to ONNX and high-performance inference paths including ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO and TensorRT per project documentation/README. Such conversions can change latency and memory, so benchmark the exported artifact on the actual target backend. [PaddleOCR deployment guide](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)

### Inferences
- PP-OCR is the strongest first candidate when small deployment footprint, downloadable maintained weights, multilingual coverage and a documented inference ecosystem matter together.
- For a realistic GPU budget, measure peak allocated and reserved device memory for the complete pipeline on worst-case image dimensions and expected concurrency; also measure CPU RAM and model load time. Batch size and detector resize policy often matter more than recognizer parameter count.
- FP16/INT8 quantization and static-graph export can reduce memory and improve throughput, but assess recognition degradation especially on small text, diacritics, uncommon scripts and low contrast.
- License diligence should record separate terms for source code, checkpoint artifacts, backbone/pretraining weights, and datasets. Treat missing or ambiguous checkpoint terms as unresolved rather than assuming the code license controls all weights.

### Gaps
- Retrieved primary sources did not enumerate current per-checkpoint download file sizes or side-by-side peak VRAM for PP-OCRv4/v5/v6, DB/DB++, SVTR, ABINet, PARSeq, SATRN or TrOCR under one common runtime. Those must be obtained from exact model files and measured on the intended hardware.
- The PP-OCR repository and README report model tiers/parameter counts and releases, but not enough details here to map every versioned detection/recognition/direction model into exact weight sizes and image-specific peak memory.
- License terms for several research checkpoints and their training data were not established by paper abstracts or repository landing pages alone. Individual release artifacts and license files require a separate legal review before redistribution/commercialization.

## What successors and practical evaluation approach should be considered now?

### Takeaway
Treat the named architectures as complementary components rather than a single model leaderboard: DB/DB++ remain pragmatic arbitrary-shape detectors, transformer recognizers such as SVTR/PARSeq offer efficient or orientation-tolerant crop reading, and Bezier/end-to-end spotting advances geometry integration. The current practical successor path is maintained PaddleOCR releases (v5/v6), while the correct “best” model must be selected through controlled tests on target imagery.

### Cited Findings
- PaddleOCR’s current release material lists PP-OCRv6, with tiny/small/medium deployment tiers and a unified 50-language model, and links public weights. This postdates the frequently compared v4/v5 releases and should be included in any current baseline. [PaddleOCR README and model collection](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR), [PP-OCRv6 weights](https://huggingface.co/collections/PaddlePaddle/pp-ocrv6)
- The PP-OCRv5 release material reports expanded multilingual recognition and specialized language recognizers; official package APIs and deployment docs provide an integrated detector-recognizer path. [PaddleOCR repository](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR)
- DB++ adaptive scale fusion is a direct detector successor addressing multi-scale feature fusion, while DB’s learnable binarization remains the essential map-to-polygon principle. [DB++ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10304), [DB paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08947)
- ABCNet v2 extends Bezier-based text spotting; in contrast, recognizers such as PARSeq/SVTR/ABINet/SATRN/TrOCR operate on already isolated line images, so they should be compared only after controlling detection and crop generation. [ABCNet v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03620), [PARSeq](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06966), [SVTR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00159)
- Recognition model comparisons are confounded by inconsistent training and testing protocols; the STR analysis recommends unified evaluation and isolates module-level trade-offs across accuracy, speed and memory. [Baek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906)

### Inferences
- Recommended baseline experiment: PP-OCRv5 or v6 with official defaults, recording detector recall/precision, exact-match recognition on detected crops, end-to-end word accuracy, latency, peak GPU memory, CPU time and missed/merged/split instances.
- For varied scale, add small-font and wide-range-resolution subsets; for rotation/perspective, report angle bins and rectification settings; for irregular text, separately evaluate polygon localization and transcript accuracy on curved datasets such as Total-Text/CTW1500.
- Keep detections fixed when comparing crop recognizers. Compare a lightweight baseline (PP-OCR recognizer/SVTR) with PARSeq and one contextual alternative (ABINet or TrOCR); use vocabulary-aware and unconstrained metrics separately to expose language-prior effects.
- Include DB/DB++ plus one curve-specific approach (TextSnake or ABCNet family) only if polygon geometry is consequential. Evaluate deployability and integration effort alongside accuracy; older research checkpoints can demand dependency pinning and custom post-processing.
- Report exact artifact name/hash, model card and license URL, checkpoint disk bytes, parameter count, inference shape/precision/backend, hardware, warm/cold latency, peak GPU allocated/reserved memory and CPU memory. This turns “small/real-time” claims into reproducible engineering evidence.

### Gaps
- “Current successor” is time-sensitive: release pages change, and this survey's retrieved official material established PP-OCRv6 but did not exhaustively audit all recent 2025–2026 academic spotting models or their licenses/checkpoints.
- No primary source establishes that a single model among the listed set dominates across all requested phenomena, or supplies a controlled cross-family benchmark covering every condition.
