# Scene-Text OCR Benchmarks and Competitions

## What the benchmark landscape measures—and what results mean for harsh live-camera OCR

### Takeaway
The field is a collection of complementary tests rather than a single end-to-end, real-time camera benchmark: ICDAR/RRC measures detection and/or recognition on defined still-image test sets; popular datasets isolate particular scene distributions, scripts, shapes, and annotation types; OCRBench tests broad OCR abilities in a VLM question-answering format. For a harsh live camera, benchmark scores are useful screening evidence, but none alone establishes robust performance under motion, defocus, glare, severe underexposure, sensor noise, video latency, or deployment constraints.

### Cited Findings

#### ICDAR Robust Reading competitions
- **ICDAR 2013** popularized a focused scene-text evaluation with scene-text localization and cropped word recognition tasks. Detection is conventionally summarized with precision, recall, and harmonic-mean F-measure; recognition reports word recognition accuracy, with prescribed lexicon settings in relevant tracks. Dataset/task details and official evaluation resources are available from the [ICDAR 2013 competition paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2013.11) and [RRC official site](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/). The original competition work describes a comparatively small, conventional scene-image test regime; it does not represent a systematic camera stress test.
- **ICDAR 2015**, “Incidental Scene Text,” deliberately emphasizes text captured without careful framing—often small, rotated, perspective-distorted, or blurred—using Google Glass images. Its principal tasks include word-level detection and end-to-end recognition; localization is quadrilateral and evaluation uses precision/recall/F-measure, while end-to-end measures combine localization and transcription under the challenge protocol. See [Karatzas et al., ICDAR 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2015.7298693) and [RRC task pages/results](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/?ch=4). This is among the more relevant historical benchmarks for incidental handheld capture, though its scale and condition coverage remain limited relative to uncontrolled deployment.
- **ICDAR 2019 Robust Reading Challenge** included multiple tasks/datasets rather than one universal leaderboard, including large-scale, multilingual, and application-oriented reading tasks. Leaderboards and submissions are track-specific; scores from different tracks cannot be read as a common ranking. Consult the [ICDAR 2019 RRC official challenge index](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/) and select the 2019 task pages. Historical result tables should be treated as dated snapshots, not current state of the art.
- **ICDAR 2023** RRC again organized evaluation into separate task tracks, including contemporary scene text and document-oriented tasks. Current/archived rankings are exposed through the [official RRC portal](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/); the portal is the authoritative source for the selected track, evaluation protocol, and submission status. “ICDAR 2023 winner” without a task name is ambiguous. Public challenge rankings are bounded by the submitted test set and track rules; they do not certify FPS, device power use, or robustness to video degradation.
- **How to use competition results:** report the exact year, track, test split, protocol, lexicon condition (if any), and whether external/synthetic training data were allowed. RRC challenge pages are dynamic and may retain historical leaderboards; a leaderboard entry is not itself a reproducible model comparison unless the submission details and test rules are known. See [RRC](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/) and the analysis of inconsistent recognition comparisons by [Baek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906).

#### Major scene-text datasets
- **COCO-Text** annotates text in everyday MS COCO images, including bounding boxes, printed/handwritten class, legible/illegible labels, script, and transcriptions for legible text. The paper reports over 173,000 text annotations across more than 63,000 images. It supports detection and recognition evaluation in cluttered natural scenes, including text that is not the photographic subject. [Veit et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07140); [project/data site](https://bgshih.github.io/cocotext/). It inherits the source image distribution and does not provide temporal/video or camera-performance labels.
- **TextOCR** provides word-level text annotations on 28,134 images from Open Images, with over 900,000 annotated words and a deliberately challenging mix of small, low-resolution, and crowded text. It supports detection and recognition; the paper reports detection AP-style evaluation and recognition metrics including word accuracy/edit-distance-based evaluation. [Singh et al., TextOCR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12462); [official project page](https://textvqa.org/textocr/). Dense annotations are valuable for clutter, but static web images do not model blur trajectories or live latency.
- **Total-Text** targets curved as well as horizontal and multi-oriented English text in natural images; word/line instances are represented with polygonal annotations and transcriptions. Detection is evaluated with polygon overlap precision/recall/F-measure and recognition can be assessed on detected text. [Ch’ng and Chan, Total-Text](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2017.250); [dataset page](https://github.com/cs-chan/Total-Text-Dataset). Its explicit curved-text challenge is useful for signs/logos, but its scale and English-centric content limit generalization claims.
- **CTW1500** contains 1,500 images (1,000 train / 500 test) and more than 10,000 text instances, annotated with polygonal contours designed for curved text. Its main purpose is curved-text detection, not a comprehensive OCR/recognition test. [Liu et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02170); [project repository](https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/Curve-Text-Detector). Its shape annotations are valuable, but do not establish robustness to harsh optics or camera motion.
- **ArT (Arbitrary-shaped Text)** includes curved, multi-oriented, and oriented text with arbitrary-shape annotations, supporting detection/spotting research. Its polygonal representations complement quadrilateral benchmarks. See the original [CVPR 2019 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Chng_Arbitrary-Shaped_Text_Detection_With_Adaptive_Scale_Embedding_CVPR_2019_paper.html) and [dataset repository](https://github.com/AstarLight/ArT). Report a release/version when quoting counts; totals vary by benchmark subset and annotation convention.
- **ReCTS (Reading Chinese Text in the Wild)** is an ICDAR 2019 Chinese retail-scene benchmark, with text detection/recognition tracks and task-specific annotations. See the [official RRC index](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/) and select ReCTS. Retail imagery is domain-specific; Chinese script and track protocol make direct score comparison to English benchmarks inappropriate.
- **LSVT (Large-scale Street View Text)** provides 50,000 training images, including 30,000 fully annotated and 20,000 weakly annotated images, emphasizing Chinese street-view text and detection/recognition tasks. See the [ICDAR 2019 challenge paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2019.00252) and [RRC index](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/). The weakly labeled subset and script assumptions affect comparability; it is not a live video dataset.
- **MLT / ICDAR Multi-Lingual Text** benchmarks detection and, in some editions, recognition across scripts and languages. ICDAR 2017 MLT covers nine scripts in naturally occurring scene images; ICDAR 2019 MLT expanded multilingual evaluation and track definitions. Detection is generally scored by precision/recall/F-measure under the organizer’s localization matching, with recognition/task metrics track-specific. [ICDAR 2017 MLT paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDAR.2017.280); [ICDAR 2019 MLT/RRC](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/). Strength: script diversity. Limitation: coverage of scripts does not equal balanced coverage of fonts, regional signage, rare characters, or every camera condition.

#### Recognition-focused and multilingual benchmark suites
- **OCRBench** is a benchmark for OCR-capable multimodal large language models, not a conventional detector leaderboard. It comprises 1,000 manually collected question-answer pairs across five dimensions—text recognition, scene-text-centered question answering, document-oriented question answering, key information extraction, and mathematical expression recognition—with OCR-related abilities assessed through answer scoring. [Liu et al., OCRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07818); [official repository](https://github.com/Yuliang-liu/OCRBench). Its VLM format tests perception plus language reasoning and answer generation, so results are not directly comparable to word-level OCR accuracy or detection F-measure. Leaderboards evolve as models and prompting change; cite the access date and exact benchmark revision.
- **Multilingual benchmark families** include ICDAR MLT (scene-text detection across scripts), **HierText** (large-scale hierarchical word/line/paragraph annotations on natural images), and **Uber-Text** for multilingual street imagery. [HierText paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04752); [MLT challenge](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/); [Uber-Text paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06857). These are complementary rather than interchangeable: script, annotation unit, lexicon, and transcription policy differ.
- Recognition metrics commonly include **word accuracy / exact match**, **normalized edit distance**, and character error rate; detection commonly uses **precision, recall, F-measure**, polygon/box overlap matching, or **AP**. End-to-end spotting requires a matched localization and correct transcription. Metric and matching details differ by benchmark, so percentages across datasets are not a common scale. [Baek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906) document that training data, evaluation protocol, and lexicon choices made scene-text recognition comparisons inconsistent.

#### Leaderboards and current results
- Official RRC rankings are the best source for challenge placement when available, but each challenge page is tied to its task and evaluation rules; RRC’s [challenge index](https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/) links task pages and submissions. For model-level results outside a live challenge, benchmark papers and repositories give dated results, not automatically maintained, apples-to-apples leaderboards.
- I could not verify a single complete, current (2026) ranking across all requested benchmarks from a common primary-source leaderboard. Many datasets have no actively maintained official leaderboard, while third-party model tables frequently mix test sets, training data, model sizes, lexicons, and evaluation code. Therefore no cross-benchmark “current winner” is asserted here; use exact official task tables and version/date for any operational decision.
- A high benchmark score does not imply low-latency inference. Reported accuracy may come from large image resizing, multi-scale inference, test-time augmentation, recognition crops, lexicon constraints, or external training data. The original papers and challenge rules should be inspected for each model’s speed/inference protocol. [Baek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906) explicitly analyze accuracy, speed, and memory under consistent conditions and identify comparison inconsistencies.

### Inferences
- For harsh live-camera OCR, use a **portfolio**: ICDAR 2015 incidental images for naturally poor framing; COCO-Text/TextOCR for clutter and small text; Total-Text/CTW1500/ArT for shape and orientation; MLT plus script-specific sets for multilinguality; and recognition-focused word metrics for transcription quality. None alone covers the full deployment distribution.
- A relevant evaluation should add a deployment-matched, held-out **video/camera test** with frame sequences and condition tags: motion blur, focus error, glare/reflection, low light, compression, distance/text pixel height, oblique angle, rolling shutter, occlusion, and camera movement. Report detection precision/recall, exact-word accuracy and character error rate, plus latency/FPS, dropped frames, memory/power, and performance stratified by condition. This is a methodological recommendation inferred from gaps in still-image benchmark scope, not a published leaderboard finding.
- For streaming use, measure stability across adjacent frames and time-to-correct-read, not just per-image accuracy. Temporal aggregation can improve effective reads but can also delay output; a still-image benchmark cannot quantify that tradeoff.

### Gaps
- Official RRC pages are dynamic and some legacy pages are difficult to retrieve consistently. A dated extraction of ICDAR 2013/2015/2019/2023 winner names and numeric scores was not independently verified in this research pass; check the exact official track page before quoting winners.
- Exact annotation totals and track-specific metric variants for ArT and ReCTS should be checked in the corresponding paper and released annotation version before reporting a number; no unsupported current ranking is given here.
- OCRBench’s live leaderboard ordering is time-sensitive and depends on benchmark version, prompting, and submitted model configuration; cite a dated snapshot for current rankings.
- Public descriptions do not provide a standardized, cross-dataset camera-condition taxonomy, video continuity test, or common latency/power measurement. A claim of suitability for a particular harsh camera environment therefore requires an additional deployment-specific test.
